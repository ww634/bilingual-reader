## Times and places

- Day 1: Ruth's voicemail is logged at 11:52; Ben was asked to meet her at the old interchange at 12:30 and arrived ten minutes late. Daniel's unanswered call to Graham was at 12:17. Ruth proposed visiting Daniel at 14:00. Preserve this order.
- Judith calls Daniel at 16:12. Shah calls at 16:50 and arrives a little after 17:00 with an unnamed colleague. A different officer collects the answering machine at 19:00. Ben reports Ruth missing that evening; police attend her flat that night.
- Day 2: Daniel reaches the modern railway station at 09:00. It is twenty minutes on foot from his house, across the canal. The old bus interchange is twelve minutes downhill from the station, turning right before the bridge: both transport sites are on the far side of the canal from Daniel's house.
- Mereford is north of Calderwick, served by train and bus. Hearing its name does not establish Ruth's intended destination.
- The old interchange is a triangle between the canal and shops, with three bays, a low shelter and a glazed operations office. Its outdoor loudspeaker announces bay changes. Works block bay one this week; a Mereford departure is announced from bay three. These are fictional local arrangements, not claims about all UK bus stations.
- The working public telephone is a glass-and-metal box at the end of the shops, near the bus exit lane and within sight of the loudspeaker. Daniel photographs it and the displayed number from outside without handling it.
- Mr Owen runs the neighbouring small shop. Ben previously worked on its lighting and timer. The café is across the street. Ruth's flat and its distance from the café have not yet been located precisely.
- Chapter 7 ends with a police-arranged appointment at Ruth's flat at 13:00 on day 2. Begin chapter 8 from this arrangement, without inventing an unrestricted private search.

## Evidence and what is still unproved

- The legacy answering machine has no useful caller-number display and no audio output Daniel can directly use. Do not introduce a convenient digital export in retrospect. Police possess the device and power supply after chapter 4; Daniel keeps a handover document.
- Daniel's interim copy is made by microphone in front of the loudspeaker. He documents the setup, retains an untreated recording, works on a duplicate, and supplies the copy and notes to Shah. It includes additional room/speaker/microphone effects. It is never called the original evidential file.
- A chair sound at the end belongs to Daniel's capture session. The rumble was already heard on the original playback in chapter 1. No later deduction may treat the new chair noise as part of Ruth's environment.
- The additional announcement wording in chapter 4 is only a possible “bound for”; “Mereford” remains uncertain. No previously hidden clearly audible phrase has been withheld. No exact telephone-bandwidth reconstruction, source fingerprint or speaker identification is claimed.
- Mr Owen recalls a woman with a light coat and bag in the phone box around a midday delivery. He cannot identify her securely, has already been shown Ruth's photo, and did not observe her departure. His delivery note has no time. Ben records that limitation. Police receive the supplier lead and will interview him independently.
- Ben already photographed the phone-box number the previous day and supplied the meeting location in his missing-person report. Daniel and Ben's two photographs provide humour, not independent proof that Ruth called there.
- Shah's team are checking the box number against call data. No result is available by the end of chapter 7. Do not later pretend Daniel alone could establish a location that police could not trace.
- Ruth's mobile was on its charger at home. Her undated note reads: “Je laisse le portable ici. J'ai besoin de réfléchir.” Ben showed police the note and phone. This supports a deliberate initial departure; it does not establish her later safety.
- Ruth showed Ben Graham's memoir pages the preceding week and said he wrote as if she had known everything. She also invited Ben to read or take the blue folder of old letters; he had not done so. The folder was on the sitting-room table the evening of day 1. Police are checking what was already recorded before agreeing limited access. Her prior invitation, not kinship or possession of a key alone, supplies Ben's permission to consult those specific papers.
- No private cassette, financial document, folder-carrying witness description or named culprit clue scheduled after chapter 7 has yet been discovered. Preserve chapter 8's first actual discrepancy.

## People, objects and relationship

- Ben is 31; his birthday was in June. A recent photograph shows Ruth with short grey hair and candles reading 13 rather than 31. Her joke makes her more than an anxious witness. Daniel knew her younger appearance; do not describe him immediately recognising her at the shop door.
- Ben is a maintenance electrician with active clients. Another electrician is covering the job he left on day 1. He obtains ordinary information without promising himself certainty.
- Ruth's cousin Elsie has been contacted by voicemail. No response or welfare confirmation from her has yet been supplied.
- Daniel and Ben still use **vous**. Ben uses Daniel's first name during the café exchange; Daniel explicitly confirms this preference at the end. They have not established nicknames, a father/son relationship or easy trust.
- Daniel plays Ben the complete untreated voicemail copy on his mobile before the chapter 7 ending. Do not stage his first hearing again in chapter 8. They recognise that Ruth planned to meet Ben before Daniel, then something changed; neither knows what.
- Graham sometimes visited Daniel with repair jobs after Marianne's death. His desk lamp remains on the workbench. A shopping bag belonging to Marianne remains on a kitchen chair. The workshop's broken second chair is still unrepaired; the kitchen chair temporarily brought in for Shah is put away in chapter 4.
- Daniel and Ben pay separately at the café. Ben saves half his sandwich in a paper bag. Their initial cooperation includes full playback, sharing information with police and admitting uncertainty, with trust explicitly unpromised.
