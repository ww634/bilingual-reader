# What He Had Come to Ask

Ben wrote at twenty past five. “Can I come round? It's about tomorrow.” Daniel said yes, then deleted the question he had added. The telephone was not a form to fill in before coming in.

On the screen, Anna was still holding her two plates. Daniel had scanned the photograph from Claire and slipped the original into its envelope. He noted to take it back the following day. Beside it, his notebook lay open at the page where he had written “Anna”.

Ben arrived without a shopping bag. He kept his coat on and declined tea. Daniel pushed the stool towards him, without putting on it the file he wanted to show him.

“My mother is willing to see you,” Ben said. “At Elsie's. Tomorrow morning.”

Daniel looked at the notebook.

“With her solicitor,” Ben added. “She's already spoken to her. She wants you to answer about the cassette.”

“I'll be there.”

“I'm coming too. She asked me to.”

Ben put his telephone on his knee. His thumb was tracing the cracked edge.

“I don't want you finishing her sentences. Even if you think you're helping her.”

“All right.”

“And I'm not asking you to explain to her why I'm angry.”

Daniel had prepared an answer to another question, the question of whether he had earned the right to come back. Ben was not asking it.

“You'll tell her yourself,” Daniel replied.

The solicitor was called Aisha Malik. She telephoned a little later, as agreed with Ruth. The meeting would not replace the steps she was arranging with the police. Ruth wanted to speak to her son and put certain questions to Daniel. She could ask for a break or stop.

“I'll take notes,” Malik said. “No recording without discussing it with everyone.”

Daniel confirmed that he would not make one. He asked which documents to bring, and noted the time: half past ten.

After the call, Ben stood up.

“I'll pick you up at twenty to ten. I've got a job late in the day. We can't spend the night there.”

“I can go on my own.”

“I know. We're going to the same place.”

Daniel went with him as far as the yard. Ben checked the time of his job before leaving. He did not look at the chair against the wall.

The following morning, Daniel took back the photograph to Claire before nine. She had asked him to come early; the costumes were already coming back, with new holes. He gave her the envelope and Helen's contact details, at her request. She gave him permission to pass on her account to the solicitor and Michael. Claire put away the photograph without asking him what it had helped to prove.

At twenty to ten, Ben was waiting outside the house. On the passenger seat, a box of connectors occupied Daniel's place. Ben moved it to the back. The parts made a sound like loose change being rejected.

On the way out of Calderwick, a diversion took them behind warehouses. Ben's telephone recommended turning round in a ditch. Daniel kept to himself his opinion on automated directions. Marianne had already heard that case.

“The yellow sign,” Ben said. “Can you check whether it says Mereford?”

Daniel leaned forward.

“Left. After the bridge.”

“Thanks.”

They found the road without further conversation. Seeing Daniel open his coat, Ben lowered the heating on the passenger side. Daniel almost thanked him for more than the temperature.

Elsie lived in a low house on the edge of Mereford. She had left the gate open. In the hall, Ruth kissed Ben, then waited for Daniel to put down his bag. He had seen her in a recent photograph. Yet he was not prepared for this woman who did not lower her eyes.

“I believed you,” Ruth said. “Even when I couldn't hear anything.”
