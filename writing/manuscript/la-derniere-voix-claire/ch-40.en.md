# Thursday

Ben had packed his charger in the bag. Daniel noticed the empty socket before seeing the coat on his shoulders. He put away the sheets he had just filled in.

“Elsie's coming to pick me up,” Ben said. “I'm going to stay two or three days.”

“All right.”

Daniel had prepared a question about Ruth's flat. He let it wait. Ben was not asking him to choose what he would do there.

Daniel's telephone was charging beside the bread basket. An officer had brought it back to him that afternoon, after the agreed checks. He had signed the return form holding the sheet against the table. He could receive messages again without borrowing part of the day from someone else.

“You'll let me know when you've arrived?”

“Yes.”

Ben checked the pocket where he kept his keys. He had his own. Ruth's were not there.

“Thursday,” Daniel said. “Next week. If you'd like to come for dinner.”

Ben stopped searching in his pocket.

“To work?”

“To eat. We can talk about something else. Or not talk much.”

Daniel straightened the edge of a sheet on the pile.

“Seven o'clock. You can let me know when you know.”

Ben nodded. His telephone vibrated before he answered. Elsie was waiting outside the house.

Daniel went with him to the door. Marianne's bag was against the cupboard. He pushed it with his foot to clear the way. Ben went out with his bag. He turned round at the bottom of the step.

“Call if you need a hand.”

“You too.”

Daniel waited for the car to leave before going inside. He closed the front door, then checked the lock.

In the kitchen, the chair where Ben had sat was slightly away from the table. Daniel left it like that while he put away the dishes. Then he pushed it back.

He took a sheet for the shopping. Marianne used to buy pears when they were still hard. She lined them up on the windowsill and forbade him to test them with his thumb. He had maintained for years that a pear bought ripe saved a negotiation. She bought them anyway.

Daniel wrote bread, eggs, then stopped his pen. He did not know what Ben did not like. About mayonnaise, it was mainly Ben who had learned.

The message arrived at nine twelve. Daniel had left the telephone in the kitchen. He got up to go and read it.

“Arrived safely. Thursday, seven o'clock. No mushrooms, please.”

Daniel wrote two people at the top of his list.
