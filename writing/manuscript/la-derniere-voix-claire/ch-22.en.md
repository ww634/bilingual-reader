# What Anna Wanted

Claire Dawson opened the door with a pin between her lips. She took it out before saying hello.

“Come in. Mind the trousers.”

A pair of black trousers hung over the back of a chair, one leg shorter than the other.

“There's still work to do,” Claire added.

Daniel had said nothing. He wondered whether the part called for a shorter leg; the theatre had its constraints.

The theatre group was rehearsing that evening. Claire had to deliver the costumes before six. She offered him the kitchen table, with forty minutes and coffee. Daniel accepted all three. She cleared a space among the reels of thread and placed a biscuit tin in front of him. It contained buttons.

“Not that one,” Claire said.

Daniel explained to her his former role and why he was re-examining it. He did not play her the recordings. She asked what he wanted to know.

“How she worked. What she was planning,” Daniel said.

Claire poured the coffee.

“You're the first in a long time not to begin with her husband.”

He took no credit.

They had met at an evening class. Claire was preparing for a change of job. Anna wanted to be able to choose her clients.

“She didn't like people saying she helped out. She worked. She sent invoices,” Claire said.

Claire cut a loose thread on the trousers beside her.

“She always forgot to invoice me. She always said we'd settle it over lunch.”

“Did you settle them?” Daniel asked.

“I chose overpriced restaurants.”

She took out a photograph from a cardboard envelope. Anna was standing beside a garden table, a plate in each hand. Someone had cropped out the top of the parasol. She was looking at the camera with amused impatience.

“She was waiting for me to help her. I wanted a photograph.”

Daniel looked at the two plates. On the left-hand one, a piece of cake was sliding towards the edge.

She searched for a moment in the envelope of photographs. There was a restaurant card with two sums on the back.

“Our lunch,” Claire said. “She refused to split the bill in half.”

“Why?” Daniel asked.

“I'd had wine. She always had dessert. According to her, we couldn't know who was paying for whom without doing the calculation.”

Daniel looked at the figures. The two results were identical.

“Was she pleased?” Daniel asked.

“Very annoyed. I kept the card to remind her.”

Claire smiled as she put it away. She did not offer to give it to Daniel. He did not need to take all the memories away with him.

Claire did not ask whether he thought she was beautiful. She told him that Anna cut cakes too soon, before they cooled. Then she spoke of Birmingham. Anna had a work contact there. Nothing signed, as far as she knew. She had started looking at rents, then complained about the price of the places she wanted to live.

“She wanted to leave,” Daniel said.

“She also wanted a flat with good light. Not just an emergency exit.”

Daniel crossed out the word “escape” he had just written. Claire saw him and continued. Anna could be afraid of Michael and miss certain days with him. She also had days when she talked about other things. Claire refused to reconstruct every conversation around the last one.

“I no longer know which day she told me what. What I mostly remember is what we repeated,” Claire said.

The recorder, for example. Anna dictated into it reference numbers, questions, sometimes the shopping list.

“She played me a list where the butter appeared between two payment problems,” Claire said.

Claire put down her scissors.

“When someone explained figures to her, she wanted to be able to go back over what they had said. Especially if the explanation changed.”

It was not a memory of a particular recording. Daniel noted that.

For documents, she kept a copy before returning what she had been lent. She wrote her questions on hers.

“That way,” Claire said, “she knew whether she'd misread something. Or whether someone came back with a different sheet.”

Daniel thought of the single set Paul had described. A recollected habit was not enough to prove what Anna had copied that evening.

Claire stood up to fetch a cookery book. A folded sheet was tucked in near the desserts.

“She had returned it to me with this.”

At the top, Anna had written “Wednesday”. No complete date. The first paragraph warned Claire that the little cakes in the bag were burnt underneath. The second discussed her work.

“Paul is bringing me his payment schedule on Thursday evening. I'm keeping mine, I want to put the two side by side. If he only brings totals, he can take them away again.” Daniel asked for permission to photograph the whole sheet, front and back. Claire agreed.

“Had you shown it to the police?” Daniel asked.

“Yes. A woman had made a copy of it. I don't remember her name,” Claire replied.

She placed the return of the book in Anna's final week. Daniel distinguished that memory from what the paper established on its own. He would request the copy retained in the file and the handover record. Claire closed the book.

There remained a few minutes. Daniel asked whether he could also have a copy of the photograph. Claire looked at him.

“What for?”

He searched for a professional reason, then gave up.

“I only knew the other one,” Daniel said.

She gave him the photograph to scan, on condition that he brought it back. He promised to do so.

Outside, he sent a message about the letter to Helen and Shah. He did not write that he had identified the man on the tape. He had a business appointment to verify, a payment schedule expected from Paul and the one Anna had wanted to keep. In the folder, the woman with two plates was still waiting for someone to help her.
