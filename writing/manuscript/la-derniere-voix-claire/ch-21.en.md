# What He Had Left Out

Daniel began his letter three times. In the first version, “it appeared” that precautions had been lacking. In the second, “we had perhaps” influenced an answer. He kept those drafts in the folder and opened a third page. “I supplied the first name Michael before Ruth Bennett repeated it.”

It was nine o'clock. Ben had not written. Neither had Daniel. He attached his preliminary observations, the file references and the processing log. Nisha's opinion would be supplied by her. He did not summarise it as an endorsement of his own text. He sent the letter to Helen and Shah, under the agreed arrangements.

The following morning, Helen called before her first appointment. “Are you withdrawing your identification?” “I'm withdrawing the certainty I gave it. I cannot maintain that the first name can be reliably identified.” “We will need to compare that wording with your report and with what was said at trial.” Daniel looked at the file that had arrived a few days earlier. It contained enough work to stop a man in a hurry from thinking he was finished.

Helen asked him to distinguish his memories from what the tape told him. He remembered the visit, the player, the page he had brought. He remembered Ruth's hesitations and her eventual agreement. The exact words came from the recording. He was not going to turn a recent listening into an independent memory.

“And my letter?” “It will be useful. It is not a decision on the conviction.” Helen needed to examine the significance of the new material for an application for review. The case contained other evidence besides the recording and Ruth's statement. An application guaranteed neither a referral to a court nor release. Daniel said he understood.

Before hanging up, he asked whether Michael had received his answer to the question that had been passed on. “I will pass on to him what you are willing to put in writing.” He would have liked to know what Michael would think of it. He did not ask. Helen had given him a task, not an appointment with his forgiveness.

Shah came at ten, at the time arranged by phone. Daniel gave her his chair and took the stool. She put a fresh notebook on the workbench. “I'm going to ask you to go through it from the beginning.” He began with Ruth's call. “From 2001,” Shah said.

He described the question he had received, the proposed reading, then his own work. When he used the word “confirm”, Shah asked him what he was confirming. “A reading.” “Whose?” “It appeared in the request. I need to find who had formulated it.” He pointed her towards the copy of the letter at Judith's. He did not know who had chosen each word.

Shah returned to his initial explanations that week. Daniel had presented Ruth's concern as a possible reaction to the book. Yet he knew she had hesitated before. “Why didn't you say so?” He looked at the leg of the unused chair. “I thought we'd helped her. I kept preferring that explanation.” Shah wrote before asking her next question.

Shah asked him to specify what he had done while Ruth hesitated. “I played the extract again. Several times.” “After showing her your text?” “Yes.” He added that he had taken her agreement as support for his analysis. Shah put her pen on the notebook. “That is what is missing from your letter.” He had described the actions and the words. He had not yet explained the use he had made of the reply. He took a separate sheet to write that addition.

His phone vibrated beside the keyboard. He looked at the screen before he could stop himself. A delivery notification. He turned the phone over. Shah gave him time to finish his sentence. When he read it back, his voice caught on the word “independent”. He started again. This time, he reached the end.

Ruth's cassette was held by the police. The copies supplied and the documents had their reference numbers. Shah checked what he had received, done and shared. He mentioned the listening with Ben and Ruth's permission. He also confirmed that he was no longer looking for where she was hiding. Her welfare had been checked. It was no longer an unexplained disappearance.

“If the old case is weakened,” Daniel asked, “does that change your investigation into Graham?” “It may change the questions we ask. It doesn't tell us who was with him.” She had the appointment note and the information about the expected visitor. The checks were continuing. She did not give him the provisional results.

Daniel spoke about the exchange concerning two copies. He specified that the word “copies” was his interpretation. Shah consulted the references for the Harris file he had supplied. “What had Graham learned about Anna's work?” He began with the pages of the book. Then he stopped. He knew more about the corrections concerning his own name than those concerning Anna.

After Shah left, he found Jo's message. Claire Dawson had agreed to see him that afternoon. Her name also appeared in an old contact list in the file. Daniel had skimmed that page to find the one bearing his signature. He took a notebook and wrote at the top: “Anna.”
