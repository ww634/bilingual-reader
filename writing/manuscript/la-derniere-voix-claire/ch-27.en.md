# Two Explanations

Nisha arrived the following day with a computer and her own headphones. She was working in the area and had set aside two hours for their comparison. Daniel gave her his chair. The stool had ended up becoming his station for difficult conversations.

“Have you read my new summary?” he asked.

“Yes. After finishing my notes on the audio. I've noted at what point I received each document.”

She took out a sheet from her bag.

“Today, we're looking at what the sound allows us to distinguish. Not the story we prefer.”

Daniel had prepared two explanations. In the first, Michael returned to talk about money and the argument resumed. In the second, Anna received someone to compare payment documents. He had written Paul's name in the margin, then added Colin's. The second name was noticeably smaller.

Nisha placed the sheet on the other side of the bench.

“That won't make us forget the names,” she said. “But we can avoid reading them on a loop.”

They checked that they were working from the same complete copy, with the same reference points. Nisha's notes distinguished the proposed words, the noises and their interpretations. Daniel opened the unprocessed file. The cursor started well before the exchange about the two.

There was paper, a dull sound, movements that no picture was there to explain. Then the male voice: “You made two?” Anna answered: “One for me.”

Daniel let the playback continue. The voices grew louder. He stopped at the agreed marker, without restarting immediately.

“The copies,” he said.

Nisha pointed to the column for speech.

“Where is the word?”

Daniel moved his note into the next column.

“There's the paper,” he said. “And the letter Claire kept.”

“Yes. Those are two different observations. If we mix them in the transcript, the next reader will think the tape says it all.”

They went back over the paper noise. Daniel placed markers on either side, with enough room to retain the whole movement as they heard it. When he enlarged the display, he could see several close peaks. They were not counting sheets. A single crumpled page could produce several noises.

“An invoice, a newspaper, an envelope,” Daniel said. “Same noise, three stories.”

He kept the marker, without specifying the kind of paper. A larger screen would not give them a better view of the room.

The man's voice grew louder after Anna's reply. Daniel had written “moves closer”. He hesitated, his pen above the word. He listened to the transition, then changed the note to “higher level”. They did not have a measurement of distance between the man and the machine.

Daniel went back to his two explanations. In each of his hypotheses, a man came to talk about money. Paper could be present in each. They needed to look for what separated them, not what made the second easy to tell.

“The letter names Paul,” Daniel said. “Ruth describes a rigid object. Michael says he was carrying a soft bag on his first visit.”

“And Colin could come with papers. Michael could carry something else that evening.”

Daniel nodded. Claire's letter still needed comparing with the old copy and its handover date. Helen had put in the request.

They took a break. In the kitchen, Nisha asked whether he had seen Michael. Daniel answered as he put two cups on the tray.

“He wants me to continue.”

“Then continue. He hasn't asked you to choose faster.”

Back at the bench, Daniel asked another question.

“A voice comparison would warrant a separate request. But we're missing a reference sample.”

“And we would need to assess whether this tape is suitable,” Nisha replied. “The duration, the quality, the recording conditions. Obtaining another voice isn't enough.”

Daniel noted the question for Helen, without mixing it with their conclusion about the words.

“I don't need the tape to give me his name,” he said. “I need to know what Anna was asking him to justify.”

“And who had to justify it.”

They kept both hypotheses in the document. Nisha added the limitations to her own working note. Daniel sent Helen the list of checks that depended on the papers.

A message from Colin had been waiting for him since late morning. He had found the file he had promised them. He suggested they go over the two versions of his schedule the following day. At the end, he had added: “I need to correct what I told you.”

Daniel confirmed that he would come. This time, he left the headphones on the bench.
