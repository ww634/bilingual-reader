# After the Noise

The nurse asked Daniel to take off his watch. He tried with his right hand. The strap had become an object of excessive complexity. She helped him, then asked him to go over what had happened.

He spoke about his wrist being pulled, the edge of the bench against his ribs, his shoulder against the door. She checked that he had not hit his head. He said no. At the next question, he started again to explain the cassette.

“For now, I'm dealing with your arm, Mr Mercer.”

He let her get on with it.

Ben was waiting a little further away. They had taken their initial accounts separately. Daniel had given Paul's words, the number called, the position of the telephone. He had asked that they preserve the sheet that had fallen in the workshop. The officer had told him not to deal with it himself.

The examinations showed no fracture. His wrist remained swollen and painful. He was given instructions for the following days and reasons to come back. He kept them with his papers, at Ben's request.

In the waiting room, Ben had bought a bottle of water. It was open. He held it in both hands.

“I saw the glass when I came back,” he said. “I heard a voice. Not the words. I called from the gate.”

He turned the cap between his fingers.

“I'd left my bag. I wanted to see you. To know where you were.”

“Thank you.”

Ben looked at the bandage.

“I saw him. For a second, I wanted to ask him why.”

Daniel did not answer that he understood. He did not know what Ben had seen in that second.

“You called to me.”

Ben screwed the cap all the way on.

A woman called the name of another patient. Ben gave a start, then put down the bottle between his feet.

“I was still angry with her.”

Daniel kept silent.

“Last night. I hadn't finished.”

He was looking at the bottle he had just put down. Daniel thought of the last conversations people corrected afterwards, until they made them say what they would have liked.

“You can still be angry.”

Ben rubbed his palms on his trousers. He gathered Daniel's papers to carry them himself.

They returned in the early evening, in a taxi. The police had confirmed that they could enter the house through the front door. The workshop remained closed for examination. Ben's bag had been recovered with permission from the officer at the scene.

Daniel stopped in front of the cupboard in the hall. He needed to take off his cardigan.

“Could you hold this sleeve?”

Ben put down his bag. He eased out the fabric without pulling on the wrist. Daniel thanked him.

In the kitchen, he asked whether he should set out two plates. Ben said yes. He took out his charger and plugged it in beside the table.

“For tonight, I'd like to stay.”

Daniel put down the second plate. They did not need to decide about tomorrow.
