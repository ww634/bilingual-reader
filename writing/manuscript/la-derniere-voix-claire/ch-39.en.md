# What Cannot Be Given Back

Daniel had offered tea to Judith. She looked at his wrist, then asked where he kept the cups. He showed her the cupboard. Ben was in the sitting room, on the telephone with Elsie.

“I've asked them to suspend work on the book,” Judith said. “For now.”

She took out two cups, then added a third.

“I don't know what to do with the other pages. The ones where he talks about the holidays. About our first car.”

“You don't have to decide today.”

Judith lined up the handles on the tray.

“Did you write what he said at Ruth's house?”

“What we can hear. And what I did.”

“Then leave it as it is.”

Judith had received an update on the investigation. According to Shah, the medical findings and those from the stairs supported the hypothesis of an assault. She repeated the word, then looked at the kettle until it stopped.

Daniel moved the teapot with his right hand. He would have liked to tell her one of the stories in which Graham had made him laugh. They queued up, like witnesses someone had coached. He let them wait.

On the table lay the note from his call with Helen. She had confirmed receipt of both reports. She was preparing the application to the Criminal Cases Review Commission, the CCRC. After the appeal that had failed, this material needed to be presented in a request for review. The Commission could refer the case to the appeal court. It could not itself overturn the conviction.

“Paul's arrest doesn't automatically change the decision against Michael,” Helen had said. “I'm going to see him with the reports and the photograph.”

Daniel had asked whether he could do anything else.

“Preserve the documents. Answer the questions. Even the ones that aren't about Paul.”

He had written Michael's name at the top of his note. No release date followed.

Ben came back to take his cup. Judith told him she was sorry. He thanked her while looking at the table. She did not ask him how he was.

They stayed for a while without speaking. A vehicle stopped in the street. Daniel looked up before recognising the postman's van. Judith had made the same movement. She drew back her cup towards her.

As she was leaving, she asked Daniel not to send her everything he wrote.

“When it's necessary, you'll tell me. Not every evening.”

He said yes. In the hall, she kissed him on the cheek without touching his arm.

Ben joined him when she had gone.

“Elsie wants me to come to her house. To talk about my mother's things.”

Daniel waited.

“I don't know what I want to keep. I don't even know whether I want to think about it.”

“You can go and see her without sorting things out.”

Ben looked at his telephone. He was going to write to her himself. Daniel went back to the kitchen. Access to the workshop had not yet been restored. On a fresh sheet, he began the requested inventory. He wedged the sheet under a book to hold it without his left hand. He wrote down the name of the first case, then the person to ask for the documents. He did not begin with his conclusion.
