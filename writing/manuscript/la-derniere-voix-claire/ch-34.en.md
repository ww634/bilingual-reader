# The Man in Both Stories

Ben had come back into the workshop. He had put his shoes back on without retying the laces. Daniel looked at the sheet they had started together. The first dates were straight. The last ones sloped down towards the edge. He had ended up writing around the things he refused to bring together.

Anna had been expecting Paul's schedule. She had kept her own. The money intended for the work had arrived in the company run by Paul. The documents then showed a repayment to him. Ruth had entered the change. Colin had removed the qualification. Their failings had protected Paul. They did not change the recipient's name.

On A, a man asked whether Anna had made two. She was keeping one for herself. The sound did not supply the name of the object. But Claire's letter described two schedules Anna wanted to compare, and the accounts explained what a comparison threatened to reveal. Paul had insisted on the single set, then asked which copies they had. Daniel had thought he was helping them find papers. He could also be trying to find out which ones had escaped him.

Daniel placed the copy of the letter beside it. Twenty-five years later, Graham had gone back over the same payments. He had warned Paul that he was going to contact Michael's solicitors. Paul had come, then had denied the visit. His second account did not stand up any better. At six forty-one, Graham was still reporting Paul's presence.

It was no longer an old argument alongside a recent murder. It was the same question asked twice, of the same person. Who had received the money, and why?

“It's Paul?” Ben asked.

Daniel looked up.

“I think he killed Anna. And Graham when he wanted to reopen that question.”

“And my mother?”

Daniel looked at Ruth's place on their sheet. Her statement had just opened up the same route. He had thought of Paul as soon as he heard the news. He said so.

“I don't yet know what happened at her flat. I'm going to tell Shah how these facts fit together.”

Ben turned the sheet to read the date of Paul's call.

“He knew she'd spoken.”

“He knew she'd given an account. That's part of what I'm going to send.”

Daniel called Shah at eleven fifteen. He did not recite every page. He gave the sequence and the references. She asked him to send it with a clear distinction between the sources and his conclusions. He confirmed that they were both at the workshop. Paul had not been found. The precautions remained the same.

After sending it, Daniel asked Ben whether he wanted to stay another night.

“I'll go and get some things. A charger. I'll be back.”

His flat was less than ten minutes away on foot, via the canal bridge. He would take a bag and come back straight away. Daniel offered to go with him.

“I just want to have a shower. At home. Half an hour.”

At eleven thirty-five, Daniel let him out and locked the door. The inner door had remained shut after their call. It led to the kitchen, on the same side of the workshop as the courtyard exit. Daniel put his telephone beside the keyboard.

Shortly before noon, he was looking for the address of a client he needed to inform when there was a knock. Two knocks in quick succession. Behind the glass, Paul was holding a folded sheet. Daniel picked up his telephone. The third blow broke the glass.
