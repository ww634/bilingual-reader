# His Own Voice

Ben arrived with dust on his sleeves. He closed the courtyard door and kept his keys in his hand. Daniel had prepared two pairs of headphones.

“Can I listen like that?” Ben asked, pointing to the speakers.

“Yes. And listen again on headphones if you prefer.”

Ben sat down on the stool.

Before starting playback, Daniel explained the filters and showed where he disabled them. He had retained the whole file, the silences, the passages that remained unintelligible.

“I can stop whenever you want,” Daniel said.

“Start.”

Ruth invited them into the sitting room. Graham declined milk. Daniel heard his voice promising that she would understand. Beside him, Ben did not move. The overlapping passage remained confused. Then the player from 2001 fell silent.

“The first name, it's Michael. Listen again.” Ben turned his head, but not towards Daniel. He was looking at the small speaker by the window. Ruth's reply came. The repeated name. The yes. Daniel had heard that yes years before. He had carried it away without what came afterwards.

“But I wouldn't have said it on my own.” Graham reassured her. The Daniel on the tape praised the clarity of the cleaned-up audio. The one listening placed his hands on his knees. There was nothing for him to adjust.

Ben let them reach the end of the visit. Ruth had to go and collect her son. A door, crockery, then the stop. Daniel lowered the volume. He waited for Ben to ask him to go back. Ben asked him where the papers were.

Daniel showed him the dates and summarised the change in wording. He had obtained Helen's agreement to show him the relevant passages. He did not spread out the rest of the file. Ben read the statement of the twenty-ninth twice.

“She signs after that,” Ben said.

“Yes.”

The keys struck the edge of the workbench. Ben put them down carefully.

“At the café, I asked you what you had done,” Ben said.

“I told you we'd gone to her house,” Daniel replied.

“Yes. You said you'd played her some passages.”

Daniel looked at the page he was still holding. Not a word was false. And enough space between the words to hide in.

“I didn't remember the exact sentences,” Daniel said.

“But you remembered having a sentence to offer her,” Ben replied.

“Yes.”

“And that she hesitated.”

Daniel put down the page.

“Yes.”

He tried to explain the room to him. Ruth's exhaustion. Graham wanting to move forward. The sense of doing something useful for an anxious woman. He had thought that by removing the difficulties in listening, he was also removing some of her fear. Ben let him talk. That was worse than interrupting him.

“You were being kind,” Ben said.

Daniel searched his face for a sign that he could continue.

“Is that your explanation?”

He did not have a better one. He had a more accurate one.

“I wanted her to agree,” Daniel said.

Ben drew back on the stool. He steadied himself with his foot before losing his balance. Daniel made a movement towards him. Ben raised his hand.

“I'm all right.”

They remained at that distance.

“So she hadn't recognised that man,” Ben said.

“I can't conclude that,” Daniel replied.

“Of course.”

Daniel felt the anger in those two words.

“The tape shows what we told her. It doesn't allow me to know everything she had seen,” Daniel said.

Ben picked up his keys.

“The limits, you always find them when it's about someone else.”

Daniel opened his mouth, then waited. He could explain that this limitation was real. He could also hear what Ben was telling him.

“I should have told you more clearly what I already knew,” Daniel said.

“Yes.”

He waited for another sentence. None came.

Ben looked at the empty space where the cassette had lain.

“She kept it. All that time.”

Daniel nodded.

“She could have brought it out.”

He did not offer a reason why she had not done so. Ben rubbed his forehead with the hand that was not holding the keys.

“I want you to be responsible for everything.”

He looked at Daniel.

“You and Graham. That would suit me.”

Daniel could have seized that opening. Reminded him that Ruth had signed, that she had chosen to keep quiet. He looked at Ben's phone, lying beside the papers. He had watched him waiting for news for days.

“You will have to ask her your questions,” Daniel said.

“I know. Tonight, I'm asking you mine.”

Daniel said yes.

“Yesterday, when you talked to me about the name, had you already heard this?” Ben asked.

“No. I understood these sentences this afternoon. I called you afterwards,” Daniel replied.

Ben waited, then nodded.

Near the door, Ben stopped.

“Don't call her tonight.”

“She still doesn't want to speak to me.”

“Then respect that.”

He went out through the courtyard. Daniel heard the gate, then footsteps in the street. He did not go to the window.

On the screen, the cursor was stopped after the end of the visit. It would have taken one click to bring his voice back. Daniel closed the playback window. In his record, he wrote what he had just told Ben. Then he removed the word “only” from a sentence about his role.
