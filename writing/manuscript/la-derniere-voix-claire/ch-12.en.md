# Lunch in the Workshop

At twelve forty, Ben sent a message. “I will be there in ten minutes. Have you eaten?” Daniel looked at the apple beside his keyboard. He had taken it from the kitchen before the call. It had mostly served to stop a sheet of paper sliding away. He replied “No”. Then he added “No mayonnaise”.

Ben arrived with two sandwiches and a box of screws. Daniel looked at the box. — For my job, Ben said. I have not done your shopping. — I have screws. — I am glad for you. He put the sandwiches on the clear corner of the workbench. A paper clip stuck to the paper. Ben removed it without comment.

Daniel filled the kettle to the second mark. He took out two cups and called from the kitchen: — Tea? — Yes. He did not ask how much sugar. He brought the jar.

Ben had settled on the stool. The second chair was still against the wall, with one leg slightly crooked. He looked at it, then opened his sandwich. Shah had called back in the meantime. The checks on the source of the call confirmed the phone box number. As for Ruth's movements after the call, the inquiries were continuing. Ben asked whether she had anything else. Daniel shook his head.

Daniel summarised for him what he could share from the solicitor's call. No recording immediately. Checks on the documents. Another perspective would be needed on his old work.

— Are you all right with that? — It is necessary. — That is not what I asked. Daniel tasted the sandwich. There was no mayonnaise. — I am going to get used to it. Ben took that answer without asking him to improve it.

A cable caught Ben's foot when he moved the stool. He froze before pulling. — What is connected to this? — The lamp. It was Graham's. Ben followed the cable with his eyes to the socket. — May I run it behind the workbench? Daniel put down his sandwich. — Yes.

Ben switched off the lamp and unplugged it. He freed the cable, then ran it round the back, with enough slack to move the lamp. He dismantled nothing. The walkway between the stool and the courtyard door was clear. — Do you do this in everyone's house? — Only where people try to trip me up. He plugged back in the lamp and checked that it worked.

Daniel went to fetch two plates. On his way back, Daniel lifted the inner door to get through. Ben saw him. — That one too? — It leads to the kitchen. It is touchy. — That is your diagnosis? — I have known it for thirty years. Ben looked at the courtyard through the other door. A passage ran alongside the house to a gate opening onto the street. — I will go that way with the equipment. He picked up his sandwich again. The door kept its fault.

Daniel had an extract to prepare for the local history association. The interviews would be put online, with the participants' agreement. The association had authorised him to demonstrate his work using this interview. He asked Ben whether he wanted to listen. — Is this another murder story? — A factory worker and a kettle. — I will take it.

On the screen, Daniel opened the editing project. The transferred file remained intact in its folder. The cuts in the project specified which portions to play and in what order. They did not erase the passages from the starting file. He showed both edges of a proposed extract. The selection had been sent by the association, with time references.

A voice asked whether Mabel Turner had wanted to stay at the factory. After a cup clinked, the woman replied “No, certainly not”. Then the extract stopped. — She had had enough, Ben said. — That is the sentence they put in the summary. Daniel disabled the playback boundaries and started a longer section.

They heard the same question. Then a voice, further from the microphone, asked “Sugar?” Mabel replied “No, certainly not”. A few seconds later, she returned to the subject of the factory. She would have liked to stay there until retirement. She had tried to get another position.

Ben leaned towards the speakers. — The question about sugar, had you cut it out? — No. It was already there. But you hear it less clearly, and the other question holds our attention. I kept the same sound. I changed the point where we stop. Ben looked at the marks on the screen. — The software is not wrong. — No.

They listened to the rest. Mabel was describing a foreman who used to hide to smoke behind a sign forbidding smoking. The sign stood barely above his shoulders. He thought that nobody could see the smoke. Ben laughed at the same time as Mabel. Daniel let playback continue a little longer.

— Are you going to keep the story about the sign? — I am going to suggest it. — It is better than your extract. — It was not my extract. — You said that. Daniel looked up. Ben was smiling. This time, he was not accusing him of anything. Daniel smiled too.

Ben pointed at the pieces on the screen. — And if I want to hear just the story about the sign? Daniel placed a marker before the start of the anecdote. He made a first cut just after a laugh. On playback, Mabel's breath stopped halfway through. Ben winced. — It sounds as though she is in pain. Daniel moved the boundary to keep the whole breath. He left a little silence from the interview after the sentence. The ending felt less abrupt.

— Can you remove the hesitations? — Some. But I have to ask myself why. If she is searching for a date, I am not going to make her sound certain. Ben tried to find the start of the laugh in the drawing of the sound. He pointed to a spike. — That is the cup, Daniel said. — A very expressive cup. Daniel played the two seconds again to let him hear the difference.

They finished the tea while Daniel noted down the new time references. Ben had to stop by his job that afternoon. He offered to come back after the job, if Daniel needed help. Daniel asked him to let him know before coming back. — I will text you. Like earlier. He gathered his things without touching the broken chair.

Daniel's phone rang while Ben looked for his keys. It was Judith. She had received his message from the previous day, the one in which he asked whether Graham had kept notes about the book. She could see them the following morning. The police had already seen the papers she was offering to show them.

— The others, I am keeping to myself, she said. — Of course. — And Daniel… bring the lamp. He looked at the cable that Ben had just moved. — Is it fixed? — Yes. — Then bring it. I need a lamp in the sitting room. Daniel promised to bring it. Tomorrow, he would go to Graham's house with a lamp and questions.
