# Someone to Blame

Paul called at four thirty-two. Daniel had just returned from an errand. He still had his keys in his hand when the name appeared. He put them down beside the telephone and took his notebook.

“I wanted to speak to you before they make another story out of it,” Paul said.

Malik had disclosed the existence of the cassette in a request sent to Paul's legal adviser. Ruth had authorised it. Daniel had been told about that letter. Paul did not need to have heard the tape to know it existed.

“Do you have a question?”

“A technical point. Can you date that recording?”

“From the carrier alone, no. We need to examine its history and the documents.”

“Then write that. An opinion limited to the material submitted. Not to Ruth's memories.”

Daniel wrote down the wording. That very morning, he had read it on a sheet twenty-five years old.

“The documents are part of the material submitted.”

“They're not on the cassette.”

“Are you asking me to ignore them?”

Paul did not answer immediately.

“The accounts can be settled. I can repay what needs repaying. Colin knows that. So does Ruth.”

“And Graham?”

“He's the one who had decided about Michael. He wanted that answer. You know that better than I do.”

Daniel felt the ease of that sentence. All he had to do was let it take up all the space.

“I chose what I wrote. I'm also going to explain that.”

“One sentence from you would be enough. After that, we could put everyone back in their place.”

“How would that sentence make the other documents disappear?”

“I'm asking you for your opinion. Not to concern yourself with the rest.”

Daniel checked the time before answering.

“Have you contacted Ruth?”

“I'm going through her solicitor. As I was asked to.”

“Do the same with Helen Ward. I'm sending her your request.”

He ended the call, noted the questions and the answers, then called Shah. She asked him to send the note without waiting. She would have someone make contact again with Ruth and her solicitor. Daniel also informed Helen. He did not try to call Paul back.

Ben arrived at around six. He had dust in his hair and a zip that refused to go down. Daniel summarised for him the call he had just reported.

“My mother texted me. They've spoken to her again. She's not answering Paul.”

Ben put his telephone beside the notebook. He loosened the strap on the chair. The glue had set. He finished the drilling, put the screw back and tested the joint without sitting down. Daniel watched him put down his tools on the right, his keys on the left.

At seven, they heated up some soup. Daniel had begun a new page for the report. He left it on the screen while they ate. Ben was talking about his job the following day. He was missing a part he would look for in his van before leaving.

Shortly before eight, Ben received a call. He looked at Daniel before answering.

“Yes. I'm still here. At Daniel Mercer's.”

He gave the address, then hung up.

“The police. They want to speak to me.”

Daniel closed the report window. They did not go back to the papers. A little after eight, there was a knock at the courtyard door. Two police officers were waiting under the outside light. The woman asked for Ben.

Daniel stepped back to let them in. The policewoman checked Ben's name and asked him whether he wanted Daniel to stay.

“Yes. What's happened?”

She waited for him to sit down. He took the chair he had just repaired.

“It's your mother, Ruth Bennett. She's died. I'm sorry.”

Ben kept his eyes on her.

“No. They spoke to her earlier.”

The policewoman repeated that she had died. The emergency services had been called to her flat. They had not been able to save her.

“It's not an accident?”

“The initial findings lead us to believe that she was killed. A murder investigation has been opened.”

Ben put one hand on his knee. The other stayed under the table.

“I'll go there.”

The policewoman explained to him that he would not be able to go in. She could stay with him and tell him what they knew. Another contact would be arranged for what came next.

“Who found her?”

“A neighbour called.”

Daniel did not ask what she had heard. Ben was looking at his telephone, lying beside the keys. Daniel was afraid he would call his mother. He did not.

The officers stayed. The man offered to inform Elsie. Ben shook his head, then said yes. Daniel moved the bottle of water so that he could reach it.

Later, when the questions stopped, he asked where Ben wanted to be tonight. Ben opened his mouth. Daniel waited.
