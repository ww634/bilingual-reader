# What Graham Was Preparing

Judith had suggested ten o'clock. Daniel arrived five minutes early and waited in his car. He had bought biscuits. At the door, he regretted the choice. They might just as well serve as an excuse as a kindness.

Judith took the packet.

“Those, he didn't like. Come in.”

In the sitting room, the lamp lit a pile of books. The armchair was empty. Daniel sat down on the sofa, in the place where the folder had been placed last time.

The new printouts came from Graham's emails. Judith had handed them to Shah. She had then asked what she could share with Helen for the review. Daniel had been invited to look at these specific pages, not to search the computer.

Three days before his death, Graham had written to the solicitor who was advising on the preparation of the book. He wanted to know how to pass on material that might change the presentation of a witness's evidence. He mentioned the first accounts, the audio work and the payments. He was looking for the firm representing Michael now.

Daniel put down the sheet.

“He was going to do it.”

“He was asking how to do it,” Judith said. “He hadn't sent anything yet to Michael.”

On the next page, Graham was writing to Paul. He quoted reference HM1847, asked for the supporting documents and proposed going over the question of Ruth before contacting Michael's solicitors. Paul had replied that he would come round with whatever he could find. No time in that reply.

“The working file is still missing,” Judith said. “He printed everything. He wanted pages for the meeting.”

“Did you see it that day?”

“In the morning. On the little table. I've told the inspector.”

Daniel looked at the table. Judith had wiped it. The papers had left no trace.

Judith drew out a screenshot of their last exchange of messages. After the call about the biscuits, she had clarified which cupboard they were in. Graham had replied at six forty-one.

“Found them. Paul's here. See you later.”

Daniel reread the three sentences. Shah had given him the car sightings. Judith was showing him what Graham had written between them. Paul had not merely forgotten a visit. His account had also shortened its duration.

“May I note the time?”

Judith nodded. She took back the screenshot before handing him two old copies.

The commissioning letter was dated 19 October 2001. His own report bore the date of the 25th. Between them, the word Michael had passed from the question to the answer. The wording “An opinion limited to the material submitted” was already present in the instructions. Daniel had initially thought he had supplied it with his caveats. It had been waiting for him.

“Had you received the proposed wording before listening?” Judith asked.

“Yes.”

“And him?”

She was pointing at Graham's name in the header.

“He was already convinced. I gave him one more reason to stay that way.”

Judith drew her sleeves over her wrists.

“I miss him. And I wish he'd sent that letter before inviting Paul.”

Daniel put the authorised sheets in his folder. He did not say that Graham had wanted to do the right thing. He had died between asking for advice and the letter he had not sent.

Outside, Helen confirmed receipt of the documents. She also had Claire's permission to give the photograph to Michael. Daniel asked that she pass on to him the complete file, without cropping the plates.
