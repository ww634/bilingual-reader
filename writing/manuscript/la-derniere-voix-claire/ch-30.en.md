# A Place at the Bench

Ben arrived shortly after six, with a strap and a bag of wooden dowels. He had asked by message whether he could look at the chair. Daniel had replied without adding a condition. On the bench, he had cleared a space a little wider than necessary.

“It still holds a coat,” Daniel said.

“Mine doesn't want to take the risk.”

Ben turned the chair over on a blanket. He moved the leg, slowly, watching the joint rather than the end.

“The screw has widened the hole. If you tighten it again, you're tightening empty space.”

Daniel handed him the screwdriver he asked for. He wanted to explain to him that he knew how a screw worked. The leg testified against him.

Ben removed the screw, cleaned out the old hole and chose a dowel to fit. He explained what he was doing without looking up. It would be necessary to leave the glue to set before drilling again. The chair would not be used tonight. Daniel brought out the kitchen one.

They ate a vegetable pie that Daniel had bought. On the packaging, it served six people. Ben took a second slice. The manufacturer did not know its customers.

“I've spoken to my mother. She's gone home this afternoon.”

Daniel put down his fork.

“She's read through her statement with Malik. She's signed it. She told me it sounded like her.”

“She's seen Malik and the police. She knows who to call. I wanted her to stay at Elsie's.”

“And she?”

“She wanted her things. Her plants. She said she'd left enough other people to deal with them.”

Ben pushed a crumb towards the edge of his plate.

“I'd have liked her to have been afraid all the time. For that to explain everything.”

Daniel waited.

“You did the same. With your friend.”

“Yes. And with my work. I preferred a mistake I could repair to a decision I'd made.”

“That doesn't put us on equal terms.”

“No.”

Ben turned the plate to gather the crumbs. Then he asked where Daniel kept the salt.

His telephone rang while they were clearing up. It was Ruth. Ben answered then asked her whether he could put her on speaker. He was eating at Daniel's. She said yes.

“Good evening, Daniel. Is he leaving you anything to eat?”

“I've negotiated my share.”

“You need to set it aside first.”

“I'm here,” Ben said. “I can hear you.”

Ruth returned to their lunch the day after tomorrow. One o'clock, after Ben's job. She suggested the canal café.

“They put the salad under the potatoes,” Ben said. “It cooks while it waits.”

“Then ask for it on the side.”

He agreed, provided she booked near the window. They spoke about a tap he would come and look at. She had kept the part she had been sold. Ben promised to check before she bought a third one.

After the call, he looked at the chair held still by the strap.

“She likes you. Perhaps she will get over it before I do.”

Daniel stacked the plates. He asked Ben whether he wanted to help him go over the chronology he could show him.

“There?”

Ben was pointing at the end of the bench.

“If that suits you.”

“Yes. But we don't eat on the evidence.”

Ben smiled when Daniel brought back the bread bin to hold down the sheet. They moved the bin. The passage to the courtyard door was clear. The chair would dry against the wall.
