# The Matching Entries

The following morning, Helen received the authorised copies. She had obtained from Malik the documents inventoried with Ruth, and from Martin the complete file he had kept. Colin had sent his own appendices through his solicitor. The originals remained with their holders, under the agreed arrangements.

At eleven, Daniel joined the meeting from his workshop. Malik was with Helen. Ben had not received these files. His mother had agreed that he should be told the findings, not that he should be given access to all the files.

Daniel began with the cover sheets. Who had supplied what, and when. He had learned to his cost that a sheet could be right in the wrong file.

Ruth's copy carried her annotation about the payment change. The dispute copy did not have that handwritten note. The printed entries themselves repeated the same reference, the same amount, the same recipient. Daniel enlarged them side by side. On one, the edge had been cut off by the scanner. He asked for the whole page. Helen found it in the complete batch. The account number ran to the last digit, identical on both sheets.

“Who authorised Harris to be paid through this intermediary?”

Helen opened Martin's reply. He had not given that authorisation and had disputed the arrangement at the time. His letter of objection was in the file, along with the acknowledgement of receipt. The later letters had dealt with the sum still owed, then the settlement. No one had needed to solve a murder to pursue an invoice.

“So Ruth didn't invent it last week,” Daniel said.

“That change appears in documents kept elsewhere,” Malik clarified. “That doesn't validate everything she says.”

They followed the reference to the bank credit, then to the entry for repayment to Paul. The signed guarantee also linked Paul to the receiving company. Three different operations: naming, receiving, using. The schedule without the qualification had allowed them to be read as one.

Daniel asked whether the old copy of Claire's letter had arrived. Helen displayed it with the handover record kept in the file. The text matched the photograph taken at Claire's. The handover had been recorded during the original investigation. That sentence about Paul had not come into existence with Graham's book. Helen placed the letter in the chronology, at the date of the proposed meeting.

After the meeting, Shah called to go over Daniel's note about his exchange with Paul. She asked him whether Paul had mentioned a second visit.

“No. Around six. Twenty minutes at most.”

“We have his car passing towards the house at around twenty-five past six. And it's still there at around three minutes past seven, on the recording from the delivery driver.”

Daniel looked at the clock on his computer.

“Both times have been cross-checked,” Shah added. “With a margin of about two minutes. The discrepancy doesn't disappear with a clock adjustment.”

“Have you questioned Colin?”

“Yes. His journey away from Calderwick that evening is corroborated by footage and witnesses. I'm not going to give you his complete itinerary.”

She did not tell him that he could cross out a name. She asked him to report any new contact, and not to seek a confrontation.

Daniel called Ben at the agreed time. He explained to him the comparisons he was authorised to summarise.

“He offered to find everything for us,” Ben said.

Daniel pictured again the coffee, the plan, Jo's card. Paul's help had often consisted in placing himself between them and the person they wanted to see. For the first time, Daniel wondered what that help allowed him to know before they did.
