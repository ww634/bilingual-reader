# Less Noise

At ten past eight, Daniel opened the folder Helen Ward had sent. The files had arrived the previous evening, after Ben had left. He had waited until morning. It had taken him more effort than he wanted to admit.

The accompanying list distinguished the complete copy of the seized recording from the version processed for the investigation. These materials came from the file retained by the defence. Daniel did not have the original carrier in his hands. He noted again that limitation in his record, along with the references supplied. He kept the received files separately and created his working copies.

Helen's letter specified the authorised use. No distribution beyond the agreed recipients. Nisha was receiving the same audio material, but her first listening would take place without the proposed transcript. He could no longer forget the words. He wrote them in a column headed “expectation”.

He began by listening to the whole recording. Before the argument, Anna spoke more calmly. There was the sound of paper, a movement, then a more distant reply. Daniel took time references without trying to fill every silence. When the voices rose, he lowered the listening level. This was not a fault to correct in a family interview. A woman had lived through those minutes.

He then compared the disputed passage in both versions. In the one he had prepared, the background was less prominent. The words seemed closer. He adjusted the comparison volume so he would not simply favour the louder one. At a comparable level, the impression was less convincing. The beginning of a syllable almost disappeared in the processed version.

On a new copy, he tried a reduction of the hiss. He selected a short interval with no audible speech to characterise that noise. The software could then attenuate what resembled it. With a moderate setting, listening was less tiring. With a stronger setting, the ends of words took on a metallic sound. He listened to what the processing was removing. Amid the discarded hiss, there remained fragments of speech.

Daniel disabled the effect. The noise returned, along with some of the consonants. He had not found a different first name. He had found a reason not to impose one. He noted the settings for each trial, including the one he was rejecting.

The old notes mentioned filtering and an increase in level. They did not allow him to reproduce every setting exactly. The differences he observed were consistent with that work. Nothing in that comparison entitled him to claim a word had been added. He knew the comfort a forger would offer. Someone else would have done the harm.

He enlarged the image of an old working page. In the margin, he had drawn a rectangle around the passage. His handwriting was tighter than it was today. He recognised the overlong crossbar of his letters t, the way he squeezed the ends of lines. A note recommended playing the passage again with the text in view. He had written it to make listening easier. He tried.

With the printed name in front of him, the sounds seemed to attach themselves to it. He covered the page. The effect did not disappear because the paper disappeared. He made a note to supply that page too. In his mind, the page had always been an aid. Now he needed to examine what it helped people do.

A client phoned him about a wedding record. She wanted to know whether she should bring the record player with the record. Daniel began an overly technical reply, then asked her what exactly she had. A record in a sleeve. Her father's name on it. They arranged an appointment for the following week. He hung up without promising that everything would be audible. The client had thanked him anyway.

Around eleven, he went to buy bread. He also bought ingredients for soup for that evening. In front of the carrots, he discovered that he was still hearing the passage. He went home along the canal, without headphones. A dog was pulling its owner towards every bench. Daniel slowed down to let them pass.

That afternoon, he returned to the beginning of the recording. This time, the question concerned the quiet exchange preceding the argument. He carefully increased the level of that section, then compared it with and without light processing. The words were not hidden beneath another voice. They were mainly quiet.

A man's voice asked: “You made two?” Anna replied: “One for me.” He kept the preceding seconds and the following ones. There was paper, but no word clear enough to name the object. Two what? Copies, perhaps. He wrote it down as a hypothesis.

The sentence had already been in the complete copy available to the defence. No one had needed to erase it. The selected extract began later. In Daniel's notes, the question asked concerned a first name. He had answered that question.

A message from Jo was waiting for him. She had found Claire Dawson's contact details, a friend of Anna's. She would pass on his request, if Daniel wanted. He replied yes, then looked at the time.

At six, Ben found the soup on the hob. Daniel told him that he could no longer defend the certainty of his old transcript. “Did you hear something else?” “Not something I can replace with a different name.” Ben put down the bread he had brought. “Then say it like that.” They ate in the kitchen. After he had left, Daniel reopened his record. He replaced “to be re-examined” with “certainty not justified”.
