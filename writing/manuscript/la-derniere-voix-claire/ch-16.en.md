# One Set of Books

At half past five, Colin Voss unlocked his office door. The secretary had gone. There was a light at the end of the corridor.

“Twenty minutes,” Colin said. “I've got a train to catch.”

He looked at Ben, then at the papers under Daniel's arm.

“Paul told me you were checking dates.”

“Among other things,” Daniel replied.

Jo had passed on their request to Martin the day before. That morning, her former husband had agreed to speak to Daniel. He still had the dispute file in a removal box. He had sent scans of a few documents, with their cover sheets. The originals would remain with him. Daniel had asked for the complete set, rather than just the selected pages.

Colin seated them in a windowless room. On the wall, a photograph of a mountain carried a motto about trust. Daniel wondered which summit they chose for unpaid invoices. Ben chose the chair facing away from it. Daniel took out the first invoice.

“Four thousand eight hundred and sixty pounds,” Daniel read. “Roofing work.”

“I can't know every invoice from twenty-five years ago,” Colin replied.

“That's why we brought it,” Ben said.

The Harris invoice bore the reference HM1847. The payment list, supplied at the time by Paul's company, repeated that reference and amount. A date appeared in the column headed “paid”. Three weeks later, Harris was still chasing the money.

“A banking delay,” Colin suggested.

Daniel laid another reminder beside it. That one dated from two months later.

“Or a payment allocated to the wrong account,” Colin said.

“Which account?” Daniel asked.

“In the books. Not necessarily at the bank,” Colin replied.

He drew the two sheets closer.

“This column says that a payment was recorded. It doesn't tell you who received the money.”

Ben took out his own notebook.

“That, I know. The client who tells me it's been done. My bank hasn't heard about it,” Ben said.

Colin smiled despite himself. Daniel took out the next report. Beneath a total, a qualification noted payments not reconciled with supporting records. Colin's initials appeared at the bottom.

“I prepared schedules,” Colin said. “I didn't certify every transaction.”

Daniel took out the version attached to a later letter. The total was identical. The qualification had disappeared.

Colin stopped smiling.

“There were several versions,” Colin said.

“What changed?” Daniel asked.

“The presentation, sometimes.”

Ben turned the page towards him.

“That's not a wider margin,” Ben said.

Colin pushed his chair back a few centimetres. He did not look at the mountain.

“I need to find my file,” Colin said.

Daniel placed the first version of the report above the other.

“Who asked you to remove the qualification?” Daniel asked.

“You're assuming someone asked me to.”

“Then how did it disappear?”

Colin placed both hands flat on the table.

“I was assured that the missing records had been found,” Colin said.

“By whom?”

“I'll check.”

Ben looked at the photograph on the wall. Daniel gave him time to turn back before continuing.

“Did you see them, those records?” Daniel asked.

Colin ran a finger along his sleeve.

“I'm not going to invent a memory to satisfy you,” Colin said.

The answer had the solidity of a locked door. It was reasonable. Daniel would have preferred it to be less so.

“You're right. Find your file. And keep both versions,” Daniel said.

“I know how to keep a file.”

Daniel withdrew his hand from the papers.

Ben asked whether Anna had seen those figures. Colin replied more quickly.

“She asked questions. It was her job.”

“Of you?” Ben asked.

“Of everyone.”

He looked at the roofing invoice.

“She always wanted the preceding document. The invoice, then the purchase order.”

Daniel made a note of that expression. Colin craned his neck to see what he was writing.

“It wasn't a criticism,” Colin said.

He got up to fetch water. In the corridor, his phone rang. Daniel heard him say that they were still there. Colin returned without a jug.

“Paul wants to know whether he can speak to you,” Colin said.

Daniel agreed. He had told Colin that he was also reviewing his audio work on the case. He had told him nothing about Ruth's cassette.

“Martin keeps everything,” Paul said. “Have you got the originals?”

“Scans,” Daniel replied.

“You'll need to check which copies were in circulation. We had one set of books. The provisional schedules, that's something else.”

Daniel asked for the documents that would explain the difference. Paul promised to look. Then he said he had a question.

A rustle preceded him reading.

“Can you certify the generation of the copy examined?” Paul asked.

“Which generation concerns you?” Daniel asked.

“I don't know. That's the question that was forwarded to me.”

Daniel thought of legal advice.

“It refers to successive copies. A copy of a copy, for example,” Daniel said.

“And can you?”

“It depends on the carrier and its history. I need to see the records. Hiss doesn't count the generations for us.”

Paul thanked him as though he had just obtained information about an estimate.

After the call, Colin looked at his watch. Daniel kept one final page on the table. Dated 14 September 2001, it gave new payment instructions for invoice HM1847. The named payee was Mill Lane Services Ltd, rather than Harris Maintenance. A note in Ruth's handwriting recorded the entry of the change.

“A payment intermediary,” Colin said. “It's possible.”

In the same file, Martin had kept a business guarantee. Paul signed it as a director of Mill Lane Services.

“Possible,” Daniel said. “We are missing what authorised that intermediary to receive the money.”

Colin asked for a copy of each document. This time, he wrote down their reference numbers himself.

Outside, Ben wanted to look through the pages again. The wind lifted the corner of the first. Daniel held the folder open while he put them away.

“My mother entered the change,” Ben said.

“Yes,” Daniel replied.

“Colin signed the schedule.”

“Yes.”

Ben closed the folder.

“And the money was supposed to go to a company run by Paul.”

Daniel did not correct the verb. For once, it was exactly the one he would have chosen.
