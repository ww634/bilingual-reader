# What Others Kept

The policewoman responsible for liaison with the family came the following morning. Ben had chosen to see her in the kitchen. He asked Daniel to stay. On the third chair, Marianne's shopping bag was still there. Daniel picked it up with his right hand and put it in the hall, against the cupboard. The previous evening, Ben had asked for a moment alone to speak to Elsie. Daniel had closed the sitting-room door. Daniel pulled up a chair for the policewoman.

The policewoman noted Ben's questions before beginning. He had written them on an envelope. The first occupied the full width.

“How?”

She told him that the initial findings of the post-mortem indicated death by strangulation. She did not add a description he had not asked for. Ben pressed his nail against the paper. He did not write the answer.

Ruth's last movements were being cross-checked. Footage showed Paul near the entrance when she had returned from an errand. A neighbour had heard them on the shared stairs. Ruth was asking him to go through her solicitor. The neighbour had then heard shouting and called the emergency services.

“She hadn't invited him?”

“What we know doesn't point that way. He approached her at the entrance. We're checking what followed against the findings in the flat.”

Ben put down the envelope.

“She'd told me she wouldn't answer him.”

Daniel kept his hands on his knees. He had wanted the precautions to be as solid as an object. It was not enough for him to know they had been taken.

The policewoman explained what was planned for the next contacts. Access to the flat was not yet possible. Ben could ask to see his mother; someone would explain to him the arrangements. He asked whether he could answer later. She said yes.

After she left, Ben called Malik. He asked whether the papers would still be used. At his request, Daniel stayed during the call, on speaker.

“The documents she had authorised for release have been received,” Malik said. “Her signed statement is preserved.”

“But she won't be there. To answer.”

“There are rules for statements from people who have died. The court will have to examine their use. I'm not going to promise you its decision. But her death doesn't destroy what she handed over.”

Ben asked who he should notify about the flat. Malik offered to explain the next steps and tell him whom to contact.

He began a message to the landlord on his telephone. He wanted to give a contact number, not decide about the tenancy. Daniel passed him a sheet to write down the address. Ben wrote “my mother”, then stopped his pen.

“What do I put, now?”

“Her name is enough.”

Ben wrote Ruth Bennett on the next line. He kept the first.

In the afternoon, Shah called Ben, then asked to speak to Daniel. She wanted the list of his old cases, the instructions received, the transcripts proposed before analysis. They needed to start by identifying what still existed and who held it. Daniel asked where to send that first inventory.
