# The Full Report

“Can I stay here?” Ben asked.

Ben was looking at the hallway behind Daniel. The officers were still there.

“Of course. The sofa is long enough.”

Daniel looked for a blanket in the cupboard. He found a cover he did not recognise, then the one he needed. He had forgotten how many things you could know nothing about in your own house.

Elsie had been reached. Ben spoke to her from the kitchen. Daniel heard “I know” twice, then nothing more. Before leaving, the officers went over the recent contacts and the precautions with them. Doors locked, no meetings with Paul, a call to 999 if he turned up. They checked the numbers to reach them.

In the morning, the blanket was folded on the armrest. Ben had not touched the glass of water. He asked the time, then looked at his telephone to check it.

“My job. I need to let them know.”

Daniel put down the kettle.

“Would you like me to stay while you write?”

Ben said yes. He typed a line, deleted it, then sent a message that Daniel did not read. They ate toast. There was jam on the table. Neither of them opened the jar.

Nisha had suggested nine o'clock to review the conclusions. Helen had told her about Ruth's death. She asked Daniel whether he could work.

“I can. Read my work even more critically.”

Ben stayed in the house. Daniel closed the inner door before joining the call. He lifted it so the door would settle into place. The courtyard door was locked.

On the screen, his report bore a version number and the date. His first letters were attached, without being replaced. A meant Anna's recording; B, Ruth's cassette. For A, he had separated the file received, the old derivative and his trials. For B, the transfer, the settings, the passages he proposed transcribing. He had kept the failures. A filter that damaged the voice did not become a useful step by being forgotten.

Nisha stopped the scrolling at a paragraph.

“You write that the exchange confirms a meeting about payments. The recording doesn't say that.”

Daniel reread his sentence. The words were cautious until the verb.

“The papers make that reading plausible,” he said.

“Then put them in the sentence. With their source.”

He corrected it. In the next section, he had described his own role: the suggested first name, the repeated playbacks, Ruth's agreement treated as independent confirmation. He did not soften that part while correcting the other.

They checked the appendices before finishing. Each excerpt bore reference points in the complete file. Nisha chose one and asked Daniel to find it from the note. He opened the file indicated, without processing, then the version corresponding to the settings described. The start of the conversation remained available before the proposed words.

“This reference points to your working copy,” Nisha said. “Add the name of the received file. The one another reader will be able to obtain.”

Daniel completed the table of references. He knew these filenames like the way from his kitchen to the workshop. That was not a reason to ask other people to find their own way in the dark.

At ten thirty-five, Daniel sent the report, its appendices and the file list to the agreed recipients. Nisha was sending her independent opinion separately to Helen. He had not co-signed it.

Shah called shortly afterwards. They were looking for Paul. She went over the words from the previous day, then asked whether Daniel had received another call.

“No. He said one sentence from me would be enough. I wrote it down straight after the call.”

Daniel reopened the note he had sent before Ruth's death. He read what was written.
