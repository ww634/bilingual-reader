# What She Had Kept

Aisha Malik had chosen a chair beside Ruth, not opposite. Daniel sat down near the window. In the garden, a sheet billowed on a line then fell back, like a breath one could not quite draw. Elsie asked whether Ruth wanted her to stay. Ruth said no.

Malik repeated what they had agreed, then opened her notebook. Ruth put on the table the copy of her letter of 22 October.

“I gave it to him,” she said. “To Graham. Before your visit. I don't have a receipt.”

Daniel looked at the date, without making the gesture of taking the page.

“Why record the visit?” Ben asked.

“After the questions, I no longer knew what I'd said. I wanted to be able to listen again.”

She touched the photocopy with the tip of her nail.

“I put the machine on the trolley. You asked me to bring a chair closer. I remember that.”

Daniel remembered the extension lead. Each of them had kept a piece of the room.

“And afterwards?” Ben asked.

“I signed. I could hear the name when they said it. I thought that must mean something.”

Ruth turned to Daniel.

“Does the cassette show that I was lying?”

“It preserves what we said to you. It doesn't measure what you believed.”

She nodded, but did not look at Ben.

“It suited me,” she said.

The tip of the pen in Malik's hand remained on the paper.

“What suited you?” Ben asked.

“People talking about her husband.”

Ruth pulled an envelope from her bag. She put it in front of her solicitor.

“I entered the payment changes. Paul gave me the instructions. At first, I asked for the supporting document.”

“And then?”

“He said it would arrive. I left a space in the file.”

She spread two fingers on the table.

“Afterwards, I stopped leaving the space.”

Ben moved his chair, which had not needed moving.

“Was he threatening you?”

Ruth looked at Malik. The solicitor asked her whether she wanted a break. Ruth shook her head.

“He said the entries were in my name. That Colin had the figures. And that I ought to remember who had given me work.”

“So you were afraid.”

“Yes. But I went on when he wasn't saying anything.”

Daniel looked at his own hands. He knew that moment when someone held out to you an acceptable explanation. He did not help Ben make her take it.

Anna had asked Ruth why Harris was still asking for a payment marked as settled. Ruth had shown her the schedule, not the reminders. She knew they existed.

“I told myself she would find the problem. That was her job.”

“With the papers you were hiding from her?” Ben asked.

Ruth took her hand off the envelope.

“Yes.”

Malik suggested opening the window. This time, Ruth agreed. In the kitchen, Elsie ran some water. For a few minutes, they heard the house carrying on without them.

When Ruth resumed, she was talking about the previous week. Graham's pages attributed to her a certainty she had never possessed. She had taken out the cassette and listened to it on her little machine. She had not understood everything. Daniel's promise, at the beginning, had been enough.

“I wanted you to listen before Graham told you what I meant.”

Daniel told her he had called Graham after the message. She closed her eyes for a moment.

“I thought he would know,” Daniel began.

He stopped. Ruth opened her eyes again.

“So did I. Back then.”

She had wanted to give Ben the payment copies, then show Daniel the cassette. After the call, she had sat under the shelter. She had imagined her son reading her annotations.

“The coach was there. I bought a ticket. I knew you still had time to get there.”

Ben kept his eyes on her.

“And the three nights?”

“The first, I told myself I would speak to you in the morning. After that, I also had to explain the first.”

“You could have left me one sentence.”

“Yes.”

Ruth asked for a glass of water. Ben passed it to her. He let go of it only when she was holding it.

“Michael has spent twenty-five years waiting for something else,” Ben said. “You kept it, that cassette.”

“I also kept these papers. So they couldn't say I'd done everything.”

She pushed the envelope towards Malik.

“I kept them for myself.”

Ben did not answer. Malik asked Ruth whether she confirmed the release they had discussed. Ruth said yes. The solicitor would make the inventory with her before copying; the originals would not leave in Daniel's bag.

There was one thing Ruth wanted to add. Graham had telephoned her two days before he was found. She had told him about the papers, without giving them to him.

“He told me he had asked Paul to come and explain himself.”

Daniel felt the next question forming. Malik asked for the words Ruth remembered. He let her ask.

“He had said: ‘I've asked Paul to come round. We'll go over the payments.’ That's what I remember.”

“And you?” Malik asked.

Ruth looked at the envelope she had just handed over.

“I asked him not to mention me.”
