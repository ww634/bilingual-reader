# The Sentence to Sign

Daniel dialled 999. Paul put his arm through the broken pane. The fabric of his sleeve caught on the edge. He turned the key left in the lock and pushed the door.

“Police,” Daniel said to the operator.

He gave the address as soon as he could. A man had just forced his way into his workshop. His name was Paul Sayer. Daniel stepped back behind the bench as he spoke.

“Put that down,” Paul said. “I just want a signature.”

Paul was showing him the sheet. His sleeve bore a tear. Blood traced a line across his hand.

“The courtyard door,” Daniel added into the telephone. “Through the passage beside the house.”

Paul went round the end of the bench. Daniel put down the telephone without hanging up, behind the monitor. He needed both hands. The voice continued, small and steady.

“Step back. Get out of my house.”

Paul unfolded the sheet on the bench. A line had been provided for the signature. Above it, Daniel read: “The recording date cannot be certified from the examined carrier alone.”

“That's your answer,” Paul said. “I'm not asking you to lie.”

Daniel edged towards the other end of the bench. Paul moved with him. He was now between Daniel and both doors.

“My report has been sent. With the documents that provide the context.”

Paul looked at the computer.

“You can send an addendum.”

That word came to him without effort. He tapped the sheet.

“It's limited. That's enough. I'll settle the money. You settle this.”

“You don't decide what is enough.”

Daniel reached towards the telephone. Paul caught his left wrist and pulled him against the edge of the bench. A sharp pain shot up to his elbow. Daniel tried to free his arm. The paper slipped between them.

“Sign it.”
