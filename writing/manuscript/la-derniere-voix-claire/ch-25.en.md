# The Forgotten Visit

They left Mereford after midday. Ben picked up two sandwiches at a service station and asked for separate receipts. Daniel paid for his. They ate beside the car, facing a vacuum cleaner promising a complete clean in four minutes. Daniel would not have entrusted it with his workshop.

“My mother called you,” Ben said.

Daniel waited.

“So I'd know. And she left before I knew.”

He folded the paper around his sandwich.

“I don't know any more which part I'm supposed to believe.”

“You don't have to choose today,” Daniel said.

“That suits you, things that wait.”

Daniel looked down. The mayonnaise was oozing out on both sides. It was not the moment to submit a complaint.

At the workshop, Ben dropped him off without turning off the engine. He said he would call his mother after his job. Daniel wished him a good rest of the day, a phrase a little too small for what it had to contain.

Malik had sent a brief authorised account to Shah, noting the recollection of Graham's last call. Daniel sent separately his own notes. He distinguished what he had heard that morning from what he could confirm.

On his desk, he laid three pages: the record of his visit to Paul, the copy of Graham's diary, and his Mereford notes. Three weeks since a call, months without meeting. Then “P. Sayer — 18:30”, with “confirm” beside it. An invitation which Ruth said she had heard about. The empty chair between these three versions was beginning to acquire precise dimensions.

Shah called back late in the afternoon. She wanted to go over Paul's words, not the impression he had made on them.

“Was he talking about not having gone inside Graham's house, or about not having seen him?”

Daniel read his note.

“About not having seen him. For months. Ben was there.”

“We'll ask him for his own recollection.”

Daniel turned over the diary copy.

“Have you found the delivery driver you told me about?”

“Yes. A camera on his vehicle recorded part of the street. Sayer's car appears in it that evening.”

Daniel's finger stopped on the word “confirm”.

“At what time?”

“We're checking the timings. The displayed time is not a guarantee. And the car does not establish who was in the house.”

She asked him to retain any new exchange and report it to her. Daniel put away the pages without calling Paul.

Paul called at ten past six. He had received a request for documents from Malik's firm. He wanted to know whether Daniel had spoken to Ruth.

“Yes. I won't give you the contents of the conversation.”

“I advised her to get help. It's good that she's doing it.”

Daniel heard a scraping sound, then a light knock. Daniel took a blank sheet and noted the time.

“There's something else,” Paul said. “The police asked me questions about my car.”

Daniel added nothing.

“I went round to Graham's. He'd insisted. I should have told you.”

“You told me you hadn't seen him for months.”

“I meant to talk about the book. This time, it was the accounts.”

Daniel wrote the two words on separate lines. Paul had just built a partition inside a sentence that had not had one.

“When did you go round?”

“The evening before he was found dead. Around six. I stayed a quarter of an hour. Twenty minutes, at most.”

“Had you brought him documents?”

“No. He had some. He showed me references. I told him I'd check.”

“Did you speak about Ruth?”

Paul exhaled.

“She was in those files, Daniel. You know that.”

He went on before Daniel answered.

“When I left, he saw me out. Graham was alive.”

After the call, Daniel sent his notes to Shah, with Paul's words and the questions he himself had asked. On the diary copy, the word “confirm” remained in the same place. It was no longer the only trace of a possible visit.
