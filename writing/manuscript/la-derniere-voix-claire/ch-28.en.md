# The Missing Qualification

Colin had chosen a table in his office, this time. The mountain had stayed in the other room, along with the trust. Daniel had reread the message before coming. Ben was working. He had asked to be told afterwards, without promising to come round.

A woman was sitting beside Colin. He introduced his solicitor, then indicated a file with dividers.

“You may take notes,” the solicitor clarified. “The copies will go through the firms.”

Daniel sat down.

“What are you correcting?”

Colin laid out the two versions of his schedule.

“I removed the qualification. Me. Paul didn't alter a report behind my back.”

He placed a letter between them. Paul stated in it that the disputed payments had been checked. He asked for a version that did not leave a resolved difficulty outstanding. A difficulty could therefore be resolved before the bills.

“The supporting documents?” Daniel asked.

“I asked for them. You have the request on the next page.”

“And the reply?”

“Not in the file. I can't assure you that I never received anything else. But I have no record of a check. I signed anyway.”

Daniel looked at the initials. In the second version, they were not smaller.

“Why?”

“Because I'd already approved the previous schedule. The one that presented the payments as checked.”

Colin drew his cup closer without drinking.

“Keeping the qualification meant explaining what I hadn't done the first time.”

Daniel wrote down the answer. He did not tell him that he understood. Colin had not asked him for that favour.

The next sheet carried the payment details for Mill Lane Services. Colin took out the guarantee that Martin also held. Same company, same account number.

“A named recipient,” Daniel said. “That's where we'd got to.”

“Here is the statement produced during the dispute. With the letter that accompanied it.”

The name of Mill Lane Services appeared at the top. An entry for four thousand eight hundred and sixty pounds repeated reference HM1847. Daniel followed the line to the margin. He checked that he was reading a credit, not a sum someone had promised to pay.

“And Harris?”

Colin opened another divider. The reminders were there, then the agreement that had ended the dispute.

“They eventually got paid,” Colin said. “Afterwards. That doesn't explain where the money stayed in between.”

“Do you know?”

He indicated a debit entry, further down. The statement gave a reference, not the name of a person. An attached accounting document allocated that debit to repayment of an advance from Paul. His signed approval was attached.

“He was paying himself before paying the supplier,” Daniel said.

“That's what the documents indicate. He presented it as a normal repayment. Harris's authorisation for that arrangement, I don't have.”

The solicitor asked that they retain the references of the appendices. The documents would be sent with their context, not as isolated photographs.

“Why now?” Daniel asked.

Colin looked at the version without the qualification.

“You were going to compare Ruth's papers. With this. I'd rather tell you first.”

Candour also had its closing time. Daniel closed his notebook.

Colin had made an appointment with Shah. His solicitor would confirm to Malik which documents could be shared. He had also reported Paul's call during their first visit.

“He asked me what you had. I told him I was looking for my own file. Yesterday, I told him to go through my solicitor.”

Daniel put away his notebook. Outside, he called Helen to give her the references. Then he wrote to Ben: “He's admitted removing the qualification. I'll call you when you're free.”
