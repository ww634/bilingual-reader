# Storing and Serving

Bottled cider leaves the worktop for a quieter place. This change of scenery does not suspend its development. Storage continues the care given so far. Then comes the moment to choose a glass and sit down at the table.

We are speaking here of sound bottles packaged after the necessary checks. Their contents remain the dry, still cider planned in our journey. Like the rest of our example, this storage is hypothetical. This advice must be adapted to the cider, its closure and the process used.

Choose a clean place protected from direct light and large temperature changes. Avoid places where the bottles could freeze. A sunny window makes a lovely setting, but a poor storage place. Cider does not need a suntan.

The chosen conditions must match the stabilization method. A cider requiring refrigerated storage must be kept under the specified conditions. The refrigerator does not correct premature bottling. Nor does it guarantee unlimited storage.

Store the bottles on a stable support without a risk of falling. For bottle position, follow the recommendations of the closure manufacturer. Caps and stoppers do not all require the same storage position. A tradition recounted at dinner does not replace your equipment instructions.

Keep a visible, durable identification for each batch. If several batches share the same storage place, separate them clearly. Record where the bottles are and the results of their checks. An organised cellar saves you a treasure hunt without a map.

No single duration suits all home-made ciders. Composition, process and packaging influence their development. Distinguish the period during which the product remains acceptable from the period when you prefer it. A tasting impression is not enough to validate its safety.

Monitor the condition of closures and signs of unexpected development. A leak, a deformation or unexpected pressure calls for assessment. Then stop serving the batch and seek advice without trying to open a bottle clearly under abnormal pressure. Still cider is not meant to announce its arrival with a violent surprise.

When serving a sound cider, prepare a bottle and clean glasses. Choose a temperature that allows you to appreciate it. Very cold, it may seem less expressive on the nose. As it warms slightly, the same glass may reveal other impressions.

Compare for yourself, without turning a serving temperature into a law of nature. Pour small amounts and observe how they develop in the glass for a few moments. You may find a better balance between coolness and aromatic expression. The right temperature is an observation tool here, not an examination to pass.

A transparent glass makes it easier to observe colour and clarity. A clean rim and the absence of foreign smells also matter. A scent of washing-up liquid does not deserve a place in the orchard description. You can use a simple wine glass without buying an entire collection.

Open the bottle with the tool suited to its closure. Handle it gently while avoiding putting the sediment back into suspension. An unfiltered cider may have a deposit normal for its process. That expected deposit must not be confused with an unexplained abnormality.

If you prefer a clear cider, pour gently without pouring out the sediment. Leave in the bottle the small quantity containing the sediment. If the style calls for a cloudy presentation, follow the guidance specific to that product. This presentation choice proves nothing about the cider's stability.

After opening, reseal and refrigerate the remainder of a sound product under the specified conditions. Record the date if the bottle is to remain partly full. Aromas and any dissolved gas may change after opening. Do not infer a storage life from the next day's smell alone.

At the table, pour small amounts and let each person choose. Water also has its place without any special comment. The pleasure of explaining cider does not require everyone to drink more. A good host knows how to talk about the apple tree as well as the glass.

Keep a few observations about this serving as you did during production. Temperature, glassware and the presence of sediment deserve a line. These details will help you compare more fairly another time. It now remains to put precise words to what you perceive.
