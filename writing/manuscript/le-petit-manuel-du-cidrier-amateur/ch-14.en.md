# Giving It Time

Young cider may seem all angles. Acidity stands out, tannins grip, and aromas are still seeking their balance. Maturation allows time for development. Yet it is not an eraser capable of rubbing out every mistake.

Let us return to our plan for dry, still cider. We have set neither a universal duration nor a triumphant tasting date. The juice, the process and the storage conditions matter. A calendar gives appointments for checking, not certificates of success.

Distinguish sensory maturation from the end of fermentation. The former concerns, among other things, the development of aromas and sensations in the mouth. The latter requires the checks appropriate to the batch. A pleasant cider may still contain sugars capable of fermenting.

Also distinguish clarity from stability. A clear cider lets light through without visible haze. This transparency does not mean that no living yeast remains. Light passes through the glass without handing us a microbiological analysis.

Conversely, a haze may have several origins. Suspended yeast may eventually form a deposit. Pectins or other compounds may also contribute to haze. Appearance alone does not allow its cause to be reliably identified.

Observe the development before choosing further clarification. A preference for clear cider is a choice of presentation. It does not make every cloudy product faulty. But an unexpected change remains a reason to check the condition of the batch.

A clarification product does not necessarily act on every cause. Choose it for an identified objective, with guidance suitable for cider. A separate small trial allows you to compare the result before treating the whole batch. Follow the dosage and the manufacturer's conditions, without piling up products at random.

Storage requires a suitable place, at a sufficiently steady temperature. Avoid sudden changes and positions exposed to sunlight. Check that the chosen conditions suit the process and the equipment. The word “cool” describes an intention, but your thermometer describes a reality.

Cold can slow microbial activity and encourage some deposits. It does not constitute on its own sterilisation. Later warming may allow activity to resume. The refrigerator door is therefore not the definitive boundary of fermentation.

Monitor the closure, the level and the condition of the vessel. Limit openings that provide no necessary information. Managed headspace and gentle transfers help limit exposure to oxygen. Careful storage is part of maturation work.

If the product is sound and can be tasted, take only what is useful. Compare samples under similar conditions, particularly for temperature. A very cold glass and a temperate glass do not present aromas in the same way. That difference can conceal the development you are trying to follow.

Write a few precise words rather than a general mark of satisfaction. Does acidity seem less dominant in the balance of the glass? Does a rough sensation seem less prominent, or merely different? These impressions are useful, but they do not prove that a quantity of acid has decreased.

A particular transformation can also occur in some ciders. Malolactic fermentation converts malic acid into lactic acid, releasing carbon dioxide. It is carried out by bacteria, not by our mere decision to wait. It can change acidity and the sensory profile.

This transformation is neither compulsory nor always desirable. It requires appropriate monitoring if it is part of the process. A less sharp sensation is not enough to demonstrate that it has occurred. We therefore do not add it as an accomplished step to our imaginary batch.

Waiting can allow a cider to become more refined. Waiting can also allow spoilage to develop further. If worrying signs appear, return to the approach of the previous chapter. Time is a working tool, not a repairer available free of charge.

At each check, record what changes and what remains comparable. Decide on the next assessment from the observations and the protocol being followed. You thus learn to accompany a development without dictating a script to it. It now remains to clarify which style of cider you actually wish to obtain.
