## Epilogue — Les mots qui sont là / The Words That Are There

### Elapsed time and legal outcomes

- Opens **eighteen months after the main action**, approximately spring 2028. This is a fictional sequence with elapsed time for distinct proceedings, not a promise that real cases conclude within that period.
- Paul's trial took place before the intervening winter. Daniel testified about the old analysis, its revision and the workshop. **A jury found Paul guilty of all three murders.** No confession or newly decoded name supplies the solution.
- The trial summary makes the physical context explicit: Anna was strangled; Graham's fall concealed an assault; Ruth was killed after choosing disclosure. These are presented as case context examined in proceedings, not a new identifying clue on which Daniel's chapter 34 inference depended.
- Financial documents, movements, findings and witness accounts were examined, challenged and compared. The first name on A remains uncertain. Do not change it to Paul, a clear Michael or a new phrase retrospectively.
- Michael's case followed its separate review route: **CCRC referral, then Court of Appeal quashing of the conviction with no retrial ordered**. Helen informs Daniel after the hearing; Michael has been released and somewhere to go has been arranged. Paul’s verdict did not itself void Michael's conviction.
- Michael has now received Anna's photograph and sent thanks to Claire. This resolves the permission/delivery obligation carried from chapters 26/31/39. Daniel does not demand a grateful reunion, a friendship or an account of Michael's private adjustment to freedom.
- Daniel's old report remains in the record alongside the correction. Further review of his earlier work continues through authorised source access. No conclusion is made that all his cases were wrong or that a single hidden group controlled them.

## The new message: source, exact content and limits

- The following morning Daniel attends an appointment arranged a week earlier, with an investigator and Nisha. He has already supplied his initial account and the references he remembers. This is a witness-context session, not an invitation to take over an investigation.
- The incoming voice message is retained from **Paul's phone seized at his arrest**. It was examined during the original investigation. The present session concerns its relationship to old correspondence and Paul's requests. No claim is made that police inexplicably left an unlocked phone unexamined for eighteen months or that technical extraction required that delay.
- Nisha has examined an authorised audio copy under a distinct assignment. Daniel is a contextual witness, not the independent examiner of his own old case or custodian of Paul's device. The message is shown in a controlled investigative setting; no informal evidence leak or public podcast supplies access.
- Checks place receipt **before Paul's 16:32 call on the day Ruth died**. No exact minute is published and the audio itself is not treated as a timestamp. The sender's identity is not established.
- Words are intelligible without restoration. There is no artificial distortion, unique voiceprint, accent-based identity, inferred location or inferred institution.

Exact French message:

> « Arrêtez de vous justifier auprès de Mercer. La formule, c'est “un avis limité aux éléments soumis”. Il cherchera une limite qu'il puisse défendre. Faites-la écrire. Pas de contexte dans l'attestation. Nous pouvons encore vous protéger. Mais il nous faut sa signature. »

Exact English message:

> “Stop justifying yourself to Mercer. The wording is ‘an opinion limited to the material submitted’. He'll look for a limitation he can defend. Get him to write it down. No context in the statement. We can still protect you. But we need his signature.”

- It supplies a tactic, pressure and a conditional offer of protection. Its match with Paul's subsequent requests turns the previously borrowed precision into a concrete new lead. Paul remains responsible for his crimes; no order to kill, coercive excuse for murder or power to protect him is established.
- The message connects the inherited formulation (13/31), Paul's early use (9), the forwarded generation question (16), and his selective dating/signed-addendum requests (32/35). It does not independently prove one person supplied all of those communications.
- Daniel recognises the requested work, **not the voice**. Asked whether the speaker was involved in 2001, he says he does not know: the wording could have circulated. The investigator's question is a hypothesis, not confirmation of an old conspiracy.
- He distinguishes what he knew before the session from what he has just heard. “We” is in the message; its speaker's identity and means remain unknown. Do not treat a plural pronoun as proof of an organisation.
- The investigator authorises a limited summary to Ben: they are checking the origin of advice received by Paul concerning the signature request. **Ben does not hear the file or receive its transcript.** Daniel gives only that summary. They can help organise the already available references and make proper enquiries; sensitive source material still requires an access route.
- Final written question: **« Qui a fourni la transcription proposée avec la demande du 19 octobre ? » / “Who supplied the proposed transcript with the request of 19 October?”** This refers to the **19 October 2001** request preceding Daniel's 25 October report. It is a source/provenance question, not an announcement that the unknown voice framed Michael in 2001.
- Before outlining book two, decide privately who sent the message, what they wanted, how they acquired the old language, what leverage/protection actually existed and whether there was a real group. None of those open choices has been answered by book one.

## Procedural references

- [CPS: exhibits](https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/exhibits) supports showing identified material to a witness and retaining source exhibits. It does not grant unrestricted access to a whole seized device.
- [CPS: material supplied to third parties](https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/disclosure-material-third-parties) informs the limited sharing distinction. The fictional investigator authorises the stated summary; this is not a general rule allowing relatives to receive all evidence.
- [CCRC role](https://ccrc.gov.uk/what-we-do/) and [review process](https://ccrc.gov.uk/how-it-works/) distinguish investigation/referral from the appeal court's power. The eighteen-month outcome is this fictional case's chronology, not a statutory deadline or prediction for readers.

Preserve the ordinary Thursday relationship and separate lives if continuing. A new investigation is an additional choice, not the only reason either man has someone to eat with.
