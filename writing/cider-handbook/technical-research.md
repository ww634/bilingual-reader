# Technical research and continuity

## Status and evidence policy

This began as the research plan for the outline. Chapters 1–5 now have a completed scope-specific source review in [research-chapters-1-5.md](research-chapters-1-5.md), with published example continuity in [continuity-through-ch5.md](continuity-through-ch5.md). Later chapters and operating parameters remain unresearched. Do not treat the outline as verified operating instructions.

Before writing each chapter, consult primary or authoritative sources appropriate to its claims: agricultural extension and horticultural research for trees and fruit; cider research organisations and technical publications for production; current manufacturer instructions for specific equipment, yeast and sanitation products; official food-safety guidance where relevant. Search and read the actual source. Do not manufacture a citation from memory.

Record the title, organisation or author, exact URL, publication/update date where available, access date, relevant section, claim supported and limitations. Mark each claim as unresearched, supported, conflicting or excluded. Resolve disagreement by identifying method, region, scale and assumptions, not by averaging incompatible instructions.

Use a second independent authoritative source for consequential general advice on packaging pressure, stability, spoilage and chemical intervention. Manufacturer-specific directions must still come from the relevant label or technical sheet. If reliable detail cannot be established, narrow the passage to explanation and leave out the actionable procedure.

## Research batches

1. Orchard and fruit, chapters 2–5: cultivars, rootstocks, pollination, site and climate, pruning, maturity, harvest, storage and unsuitable fruit.
2. Preparation and measurement, chapters 6–9: materials, cleaning versus sanitation, machinery, bought juice, extraction, instrument use, acidity versus pH, and blend trials.
3. Fermentation and maturation, chapters 10–14: yeast, nutrients, temperature, gas release, sampling, fermentation progress, faults, transfers and oxygen.
4. Finishing, chapters 15–18: residual sugar, stability, carbonation, packaging and storage. This is a separate research gate, not an extrapolation from a quiet airlock.
5. Culture and tasting, chapters 1, 19–20 and occasional asides: definitions, regional practices, history, sensory terminology and pairing examples.

## Claims that need particular care

- Do not infer fermentation completion from time, sweetness or visible bubbles alone.
- Do not treat pH and titratable acidity as interchangeable measurements.
- Distinguish bitterness, acidity and astringency consistently.
- Distinguish cleaning from sanitation, with product-specific concentrations and contact times researched before inclusion.
- Do not imply refrigeration, racking, clarity or a single additive universally prevents renewed fermentation.
- Do not imply that fermenting unsuitable fruit guarantees a safe product.
- Do not suggest tasting suspect material to determine safety.
- Preserve the difference between a general explanation of carbonation and a complete validated procedure.
- Never provide a universal sugar dose divorced from volume, residual fermentables, temperature, desired carbonation and container limits.
- No improvised heating of sealed bottles. Advanced stabilization, filtration and traditional fermentation-control methods must not be condensed into inviting but incomplete instructions.
- Keep explanatory metaphors out of the place where exact conditions belong.

These are authoring checks. The prose should explain relevant constraints at the point of use, without repeatedly interrupting the reader with generic warnings.

## Terminology seeds

These are working contextual equivalents to verify during drafting, not a finished app vocabulary file.

- *un verger* — an orchard; *un pommier* — an apple tree.
- *un porte-greffe* — a rootstock; *la taille* — pruning in orchard context.
- *la récolte* — harvest or crop, depending on the sentence.
- *le broyage* — milling/crushing; *le pressurage* — pressing.
- *le marc* — pomace in apple-processing context.
- *un densimètre* — a hydrometer in the intended measurement context.
- *les lies* — lees; *le soutirage* — racking.
- *l'assemblage* — blending or a blend, according to function.
- *tranquille* — still when describing cider.
- *doux* — sweet when describing cider; distinguish a sensory adjective from any regulated style category.

Add a term's first explanatory chapter and later reuse as drafting proceeds. Keep variety names, regional categories and historical claims uncommitted until verified.
