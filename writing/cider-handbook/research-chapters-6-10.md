# Research record — chapters 6–10

Reviewed 21 October 2026. Supports workspace preparation, milling/pressing, basic juice measurements, pre-fermentation blend trials and selected-yeast preparation. The chapters teach decisions and technique; they do not prescribe a universal fruit-treatment, nutrient, acid-adjustment or bottling recipe. No actual batch was made or measured.

## Cleaning, materials and juice selection

- [Five Star Chemicals: Star San](https://fivestarchemicals.com/star-san). Current manufacturer instructions, accessed 21 October 2026; update date not displayed. Supports cleaning before sanitising, product-specific dilution/contact time and the conditional meaning of no-rinse. The chapter does not reproduce a concentration or contact time as a rule for other products.
- [University of Maine Cooperative Extension: Safe Home Made Cider](https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/4191e/). Jim Schupp, revised by Jason Bolton; copyright 2008, 2011. Cleaning/sanitising definitions, clean food-contact collection equipment and fruit exclusion corroborate the introductory hygiene route. This publication concerns unfermented US cider; its preservation and regulatory instructions are not adopted for fermentation.
- [Penn State Extension: Production—Beer, Hard Cider and Distilled Spirits](https://extension.psu.edu/food-safety-and-quality/beer-hard-cider-and-distilled-spirits/production). Indexed page text reviewed; direct access previously returned 403. Supports pasteurised preservative-free purchased juice as a starting option and the basic equipment functions. No automatic timetable or generic sulfite treatment copied.
- [FDA: What You Need to Know About Juice Safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-juice-safety). Reused for fresh-juice limitations and appropriate washing. Appearance, filtering, selected yeast and washing are not presented as food-safety validation.

## Milling and pressing

- [Vigo Presses: Instructions for the Speidel Apple Mill](https://vigopresses.co.uk/index.php?attachment_id=23&dispatch=attachments.getfile). Manufacturer instructions, copyright 2023, safety/operation/cleaning sections. Supports stable placement, guarding, disconnection before opening/clearing and prompt cleaning. The text tells readers to follow their own machine's instructions and does not generalise the Speidel's cleaning-with-motor-running step.
- [Vigo Presses: 9 Litre Spindle Press](https://vigopresses.co.uk/9-litre-spindle-press/). Current manufacturer usage text, indexed; update date not displayed. Supports prepared pulp, bags/plates and observing the particular press's assembly/capacity instructions. No press force or pressure limit is invented.
- [WSU Extension: Cider Making](https://s3.wp.wsu.edu/uploads/sites/2073/2023/09/Hard-Cider-Making.pdf). Ginny Bode, dated 15 September 2023. Reviewed for separation of milling and pressing, food-grade collection and equipment functions. This source contains broader claims about windfalls and fermentation as a kill step that the handbook deliberately does not adopt; the earlier sound-fruit route and validated treatment requirements remain.
- [Oregon State University Extension: Preserving Foods—Fruit Juice and Apple Cider](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/sp-50-455-preserving-foods-fruit-juice-apple-cider). Indexed juice-preparation text. Corroborates milling/grinding before extraction. No canning or heating procedure reproduced.

Yield example is original arithmetic: a hypothetical six litres from ten kilograms equals 0.6 litres per kilogram. This is not a measured yield, expectation or conversion from kilograms to litres by mass.

## Measurement

- [Anton Paar: Density and Density Measurement](https://wiki.anton-paar.com/au-en/density-and-density-measurement/). Current manufacturer technical article; date not displayed. Supports specific gravity as a density ratio and the importance of temperature. No density-unit value is substituted for the dimensionless relative-density scale.
- [Stevenson Reeves: How to Read a Hydrometer](https://stevensonreeves.co.uk/howto.htm). Manufacturer guide, copyright 2006. Supports clean equipment, freely floating instrument, bubbles, eye-level reading and temperature correction. Meniscus instructions remain model-specific; not all hydrometers use the same meniscus convention.
- [Stevenson Reeves: Specialised Hydrometers](https://www.stevenson-reeves.co.uk/hydrometers/specialised.htm). Manufacturer descriptions include brewers' specific-gravity instruments calibrated at 20/20 °C. This supports the explicitly qualified statement that some instruments use twenty degrees, not a claim about all models.
- [Anton Paar: EasyDens and SmartRef](https://www.anton-paar.com/fi-en/products/details/alcohol-analyzing-bundle-easydens-and-smartref-combo/). Current manufacturer explanation of density/refractive index and alcohol corrections. Supports the limitation of a raw refractometer reading after fermentation begins. No correction equation adopted.
- [Hanna Instruments: What pH Should Wine Be?](https://knowledge.hannainst.com/en/knowledge/what-ph-should-wine-be). Manufacturer guidance, indexed text. Supports using appropriate calibration buffers. No grape-wine pH target generalised to cider.
- [Hanna Instruments: Total Titratable Acidity in Wine, Method HI3204EN](https://www.hannainst.com/hubfs/product-manuals/MAN901W_03_18.pdf). Manufacturer method, wine-methods revision 3.0, indexed excerpt. Supports that titratable acidity is procedure/endpoint dependent and reported against a reference acid. Wine's tartaric-acid reporting is not silently treated as apple juice's malic-acid equivalent.
- [Penn State Extension: Basics on Wine Acidity via Clark Smith](https://extension.psu.edu/basics-on-wine-acidity-in-wine-via-clark-smith). Indexed distinction between pH and titratable acidity; direct retrieval returned 403. Used for the qualitative distinction only. No titration recipe or acidity correction prescribed.
- [WSU: Cultivar Performance Database](https://cider.wsu.edu/ciderweb/). Juice attributes report titratable malic acid, pH and specific gravity separately; supports the apple-juice reporting vocabulary.

The 1.050 reading is hypothetical, not five per cent alcohol or a sugar-only chemical analysis. pH is not arithmetically averaged to predict a blend. No single reading is treated as a safety certificate.

## Yeast, nutrition and the numerical example

- [Fermentis: SafCider AB-1](https://fermentis.com/en/product/safcider-ab-1/). Live manufacturer product page, read 21 October 2026; page update date not displayed. Current first-fermentation recommendation is 20–30 g/hL; temperature range 10–30 °C. Direct inoculation involves a sufficient must quantity (at least ten times the yeast weight), gentle stirring and homogenisation. Ten litres of juice comfortably exceeds that minimum for the illustrative 2.5 g dose. These are product-specific facts; the chapter does not recommend exceeding them or extrapolating to other strains.
- [Fermentis AB-1 technical sheet, revision October 2022, hosted by Castle Malting](https://www.castlemalting.com/Publications/YeastProducts/Yeast_Fermentis_Safcider_AB1_EN.pdf). Manufacturer-authored older sheet, indexed. Gives 10–20 g/hL for first fermentation. **Conflict resolved:** use the live manufacturer's 20–30 g/hL recommendation for the explicitly dated teaching example; do not average old and new doses. Readers must check their actual product's current instructions.
- [Penn State Extension: Why, When, and How to Measure YAN](https://extension.psu.edu/why-when-and-how-to-measure-yan). Indexed source text; direct access 403. Supports analysis-led yeast-assimilable-nitrogen decisions and use of a competent laboratory. No grape-wine nitrogen target applied to cider.
- [Cornell Hard Cider Resources: Publications](https://hardcider.cals.cornell.edu/publications/). Cider-production research bibliography, including Song et al. 2020 and Ma et al. 2018. Corroborates that apple-juice YAN and processing conditions are active cider-specific variables. The bibliography was not treated as a substitute for reading the studies or as a basis for a nutrient dose.

Checked arithmetic: 20–30 grams per 100 litres is 2–3 grams per 10 litres. The illustrative 2.5 grams is inside that interval. Twenty degrees is a chosen illustrative working temperature inside the manufacturer's range, not a validated universal optimum. Actual dose scales with the volume remaining after sampling. No invented YAN result is used to validate the example.

## Blending and remaining gates

The A/B tasting trials are separate from the main batch. Their arithmetic is original: 75 mL plus 25 mL makes 100 mL; scaling 75:25 to ten litres gives 7.5 and 2.5 litres. Flavour preference does not establish the resulting fermented balance or safety. Samples are kept out of the fermentation batch.

Still pending: the reader's actual juice measurements, nutrient decision, vessel and gas-management instructions for their equipment, real fermentation observations, and all finishing/stability/packaging procedures. Chapters 11 onward must research these within their own scope. No precise time to completion or bottle-ready status has been established.
