# Research record: chapters 16–20

Reviewed 21 October 2026. Scope: finished-cider blending, dry/still packaging, storage and service, sensory description and reviewing the next experiment. Chapters 1–15 remain published without changes. This completes the planned twenty-chapter handbook.

## Sources and boundaries

**Iowa State University Extension, Maureen Moroney, “Bench Trials,” 24 April 2019.** [Full article](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/wine/bench-trials-0). Supports assessing a small measured trial before changing an entire batch. Chapter 16 uses its own cider-blending illustration and arithmetic, not the article's additive formulations or grape-wine targets.

**Scott Laboratories, “Bench Trial Protocol,” updated February 2025.** [Full page](https://scottlab.com/bench-trial-protocol). Supports repeating small trials for each actual matrix rather than assuming an earlier result transfers unchanged. No product-specific dose or additive is recommended. Blending finished cider remains distinct from the pre-fermentation juice exercise in chapter 9.

**AWRI, “Microbiological stability—sterility check,” undated web resource.** [Full article](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/winemaking_resources/storage-and-packaging/pre-packaging-preparation/microbiological-stability-sterility-check/). Supports distinguishing fermentation completion from packaging stability and using appropriate microbiological assessment. Chapter 17 refers to laboratory help where completion or stability is uncertain. Wine tolerances, microbial counts, incubation times and filtration specifications are not copied into cider instructions. No isolated hydrometer reading or calendar rule is made a guarantee.

**IFPC, “Hygiène,” undated web resource.** [Full page](https://www.ifpc.eu/hygiene/). Supports hygiene throughout cider processing; it does not make sanitation equivalent to sterilization. Chapter 17 reuses the earlier distinction between cleaning and sanitation. Actual product concentrations, contact times, rinsing and equipment treatment remain dependent on the relevant instructions reviewed for that equipment/product.

**French cider industry / IFPC, Guide de bonnes pratiques et d'hygiène et guide d'application des principes HACCP à la fabrication du cidre et du poiré**, modified 1 March 2018. [Full PDF](https://www.ifpc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/GBPH-cidre_modifie-180228.pdf). Revisited packaging compatibility and pressure discussion, product-dependent storage guidance (printed p. 40), and pre-packaging/batch records (pp. 156–160). Used narrowly: containers must suit conditions; later microbial activity can change pressure; storage advice depends on the stabilization process. No old legal definition, commercial shelf life, laboratory procedure, universal headspace, fermentation stop or pasteurization schedule is adopted.

The IFPC professional guide and AWRI microbiological resource independently support the cautious pressure/stability discussion. Chapter 17 gives the practical sequence for a product already established as ready, rather than inventing a safe universal final density. Where readiness cannot be established from the batch record, a competent cider adviser and relevant residual-sugar/microbial analysis are the next step. Strong bottles never substitute for controlled fermentation.

**AWRI, Advanced Wine Assessment Course notes**, filename dated 28 September 2022, hosted February 2023. [Full 46-page PDF](https://www.awri.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/01-AWAC-Course-Notes-28092022-1.pdf). Read the distinctions among smell, taste and tactile perception; astringency (printed p. 13); and sample/glass/temperature comparability (pp. 23–25). Supports a short explanation of retronasal aroma and keeping acidity, bitterness and astringency distinct. We do not import wine-judging scores, a fixed serving temperature or analytical conclusions from perceived warmth. Original descriptions and food-pairing comparisons are clearly possibilities, not universal outcomes.

**Existing research through chapter 15** remains relevant for oxygen management, measurement limitations, cold not being sterilization, suspect products not being tasted for safety, and supplier-specific yeast instructions. No new chemical treatment, nutrient product, dosage or carbonation method is introduced.

A search-indexed AWRI note on smoke sensory panels returned a 404 when opened; it is not claimed as a read source or used to establish a procedure. Historical cider.org.uk bottling calendars and early-bottling recipes were excluded. The handbook gives no warm sealed-bottle experiment, universal priming dose or improvised bottle-heating method.

## Examples and calculations

Chapter 16 introduces **two separate imaginary finished ciders, A and B**, assumed sound, dry and from completed fermentations solely to explain trial design. They are not additions to the main batch. Three 50 mL trials: 40+10, 30+20 and 25+25 mL. The first is 80:20; an independent hypothetical 5 L preparation therefore uses 4 L A + 1 L B. All arithmetic was checked. Taste preference does not establish packaging stability; the final mixture requires its own assessment, and pH is not a simple arithmetic average.

Chapters 17–18 do not invent an achieved bottling date, number of bottles, real yield or shelf life for the main example. Chapter 19's three-sentence tasting portrait is explicitly a phrasing example, not a predicted result. Chapter 20 distinguishes a teaching journey from an actual recorded fermentation, and labels the suggested personal review as fictitious.
