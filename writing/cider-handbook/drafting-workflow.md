# Drafting and review workflow

## Before each chapter

Read the brief, outline entry, editorial guide and current technical record. Review the preceding chapter when it exists. Identify the practical question, the learner's likely vocabulary burden, the essential distinction, the continuing-example state and the claims requiring research.

Complete the relevant research gate before drafting actionable guidance. Planning completion does not mean the technical review has been completed.

## Draft

Write a natural French chapter first. Open on an observation or useful question, explain the necessary concepts, develop a concrete example, and close with a concise takeaway and a natural connection forward. Vary this movement rather than mechanically repeating a template.

Break paragraphs where the thought changes. Give a new technical term room to settle before introducing another. Include sensory detail and humour where they help, with no mandatory count.

Then write natural English translations. Compare them for instructional equivalence as well as literary quality. Keep the French readable independently.

## Review before encoding

- Practical clarity: the reader understands what is being done, why, under which conditions, and what cannot yet be concluded.
- Evidence: factual claims, anecdotes, named varieties and numbers have reviewed sources; uncertainty is explicit.
- Learning: new terms are introduced in context, earlier terms recur meaningfully, pronouns are clear and sentence structure remains accessible.
- Voice: warmth and specificity survive; jokes and metaphors do not obscure procedure.
- Continuity: batch volume, method, equipment, chronology, units and intended style agree across chapters.
- Format: all essential content works as ordinary paragraphs; no dependency on visual layout.
- Translation: quantities, negation, probability, conditions and technical distinctions match.

Read the French aloud or perform a deliberate rhythm review. Remove repeated sentence openings, overlong explanations and forced jokes. Review adjacent chapters for duplicated definitions and missing prerequisites.

## Future app publication

When chapter writing and publication are requested, follow the existing French pipeline rather than introducing a new format:

1. Prepare book metadata and chapter markup under `content/books/le-petit-manuel-du-cidrier-amateur/_src/`, using the actual builder's format.
2. Keep French and English manuscript reading copies under a matching `writing/manuscript/` directory if following the established project convention.
3. Author and review the book's own contextual vocabulary resources. Reuse the established schema, not another book's character-specific senses.
4. Inspect the current builder and validation tools before running them: the builder also writes catalogue metadata and creates a cover. Preserve unrelated user changes and published books.
5. Validate pair completeness, paragraph boundaries, alignment coverage and lookup offsets, then inspect representative reader passages with English shown and hidden.
6. Run applicable existing content/lookup checks; inspect their scope because some target the earlier French book explicitly. Adapt validation to this book rather than treating an unrelated passing test as coverage.
7. Record actual validation results and review any catalogue diff before publication.

No app content is generated in the planning phase. The present task ends with these guiding documents.

## After each chapter

Update the technical source entries, terminology first-use notes and continuing-example record. Record any deliberate outline changes and review their effects on later chapters.

Chapter handoff:

- Chapter number and title.
- Draft status and French word count.
- Concepts taught and vocabulary introduced.
- Earlier vocabulary revisited.
- Example facts established.
- Sources verified and unresolved claims.
- French/English review status.
- App encoding and validation status, if applicable.
- Required setup for the following chapter.

Review the whole plan after chapters 5, 10 and 15. At completion, review the entire French manuscript and translations for terminology, method consistency, repeated explanations and whether the book remains an enjoyable language-learning read.
