# Book brief

## Promise

Help an adult French learner understand cider from the apple tree to the finished glass, while enjoying warm, observant nonfiction. The reader should gain both practical understanding and useful French for describing sensations, comparing choices, following a process and explaining a problem.

The guide is substantial enough to respect an enthusiast's curiosity, but it must remain enjoyable without a press, orchard or intention to make alcohol. Reading and sensory observation are valid ways to participate. No chapter should depend on doing a practical assignment.

## Audience and scale

Working language target: B1–B2, supported by English translations and contextual word lookups. This is an editorial target, not a certified CEFR assessment. Make the opening chapters especially welcoming; retain precise specialist terms when explained in ordinary French.

Plan 20 chapters of approximately 900–1,200 French words, about 18,000–24,000 French words overall. English is additional. Split an overloaded chapter rather than compressing essential conditions into dense prose. The user has not requested a strict word count.

## Approach

Write mainly to « vous », with occasional inclusive « on ». The guide's narrator is an informed, approachable companion, without an invented professional biography or claims of personal experience. Use hypothetical examples openly.

Follow one illustrative small batch through the production chapters. Its working specification is ten litres of starting juice, a selected cider yeast and an intended dry, still cider. Ten litres is an editorial scale, not a guarantee of finished yield or a vessel-size prescription. Record losses and any examples consistently. Research must establish the actual procedure before drafting instructions.

Introduce sparkling, sweet and traditional approaches as distinct choices with their own requirements. The first batch should not silently switch methods midway. It can demonstrate a restrained, satisfying result without treating dryness or clarity as universal measures of quality.

Cover orchard care in enough depth to explain the fruit, while giving bought apples and suitable purchased juice a legitimate place. Use French cider culture as an anchor, with regional examples verified individually. Do not make all French cider synonymous with one region or method.

## What makes it engaging

Give each chapter a tangible question: which fruit to choose, what a measurement means, why a smell has changed, whether the next step is justified. Move from a concrete observation to an explanation, then a usable decision.

Vary the opening: a basket of mixed apples, a misunderstood label, a pruning choice, a deceptively quiet vessel. These are short teaching situations, not a fictional subplot. Let satisfaction come from noticing more and understanding why.

Use scents, tastes and textures precisely. Describe the physical impression and possible interpretation without presenting sensory perception as an instrument or a safety test. Humour should arise from sticky equipment, impatient humans, ambiguous words and ordinary practical frustrations. Affection for the craft matters more than joke frequency.

## Scope boundaries

Include practical tips, common mistakes, grounded science, occasional verified historical or cultural facts, and sensory language. Explain advanced processes enough to help readers recognise their complexity.

Exclude distillation, commercial production compliance, unsupported health claims and improvised advanced procedures. Do not promise complete training in orchard pathology, laboratory analysis or commercial packaging.

The reader-facing book is continuous text with chapter titles and paragraph breaks. No tables, diagrams, sidebars, printable records, embedded quizzes, custom glossary interface or app feature development. Explain terminology where encountered, then reuse it; any concluding vocabulary recap must work as ordinary prose.

## Success

A chapter succeeds when it reads naturally in French, teaches a useful distinction, offers a properly qualified practical insight, and rewards curiosity. The whole book succeeds when the reader can explain the process and its main decisions in French, understand the limits of the introductory route, and find pleasure in the details.
