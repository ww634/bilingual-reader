# Research record: chapters 11–15

Reviewed 21 October 2026. Scope: monitoring, troubleshooting, racking, maturation and distinguishing cider styles. These chapters explain decisions; they do not supply a complete sweet-cider stabilization or sparkling-cider production procedure. Earlier instrument, sanitation and manufacturer-specific research remains applicable.

## Sources read and use limits

- **Fermentis, “Exploring cider profiles.”** Manufacturer research with IFPC experimental fermentations; no publication date displayed. [Full page](https://fermentis.com/en/knowledge-center/expert-insights/cider/exploring-cider-profiles/). Supports the limited point that strain and conditions affect fermentation performance and sensory results. Controlled trials do not predict our imagined batch. We import no experimental nutrient, residual sugar or temperature targets. The chapter-10 AB-1 directions remain the reference for that example; chapter 11 introduces no new dose.
- **AWRI, “Stuck ferments need a rapid response,” 19 April 2016.** [Full bulletin](https://www.awri.com.au/information_services/ebulletin/2016/04/19/stuck-ferments-need-a-rapid-response/). Supports prompt, evidence-led investigation of a confirmed fermentation problem. Its vintage-specific causes and wine rescue conditions are not a cider recipe; chapter 12 gives no restart formulation.
- **AWRI, “Copper” FAQ.** No page publication date displayed. [Full page](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/winemaking_resources/frequently_asked_questions/copper/). Supports chapter 12's distinction between noticing sulphurous aromas and selecting a treatment. Copper can affect aroma and stability; uncontrolled copper contact is not a remedy. We omit every treatment rate and wine-specific nitrogen threshold. No coin, pipe, automatic nutrient addition or blanket aeration advice is given.
- **AWRI, “Gases—we have you covered,” September 2023, issue 716.** [Two-page technical article](https://www.awri.com.au/files/attachment/s2370-gases/). Supports limiting oxygen exposure during storage and recognising that gas protection depends on application and conditions. Chapters 13–14 recommend suitable vessel sizing and calm transfers. No permanent CO₂ blanket, home gas dosing protocol or numerical headspace threshold is claimed.
- **AWRI, project 3.4.2, “Reducing wine movements during production.”** Undated project summary. [Full page](https://www.awri.com.au/research_and_development/rde-plan/projects/project-3-4-2/). Supports discussing clarification transfers as operations with labour and product losses. It does not establish a mandatory home-cider racking date.
- **Andrew Lea, “The Science of Cidermaking,” part 3.** Historical specialist explanation; page links to a later book and includes dated prices and practices. [Full page](https://www.cider.org.uk/part3.htm). Used for qualitative cider context: sediment separation, evolution during maturation and the possibility of bacterial malolactic conversion. No historical sulphite dosage, minimum alcohol argument, bottling density, cleaning recipe or maturation duration is imported. Instructions elsewhere on this page must not be treated as the handbook's process.
- **Andrew Lea, “Keeving—What's That?”** Historical specialist page including 2008 supplier references. [Full page](https://cider.org.uk/keeving.html). Chapter 15 uses only a short explanation of pectin-associated separation and slower fermentation. The page's assurance about excessive refermentation and its density-led bottling suggestions are deliberately excluded. Nutrient limitation is not a packaging-safety guarantee. No keeving formulation, additive dose or schedule is included.
- **AWRI, “Chemical options for microbiological stabilisation.”** Page cites work and standards through at least 2020; no displayed page date. [Full page](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/winemaking_resources/storage-and-packaging/pre-packaging-preparation/microbiological-stabilisation/). Supports the narrow explanation that stabilization depends on the product and an integrated process; sorbate inhibits growth rather than killing yeast. Chapters 14–15 do not turn refrigeration or a single additive into a universal stop. Wine-specific legal limits, specialized chemicals, filter specifications and dosing procedures are omitted.
- **French cider industry / IFPC, Guide de bonnes pratiques et d'hygiène et guide d'application des principes HACCP à la fabrication du cidre et du poiré**, modified version 1 March 2018, hosted in a December 2024 path. [170-page PDF](https://www.ifpc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/GBPH-cidre_modifie-180228.pdf). Read the equipment/packaging sections (printed pp. 19–20), pressure hazard discussion (pp. 115–117), and the purpose of stability assessments (pp. 162–163). Independent cider-specific corroboration for the pressure and stability distinctions in chapter 15: fermentation in a closed package can increase pressure, and container/closure suitability matters. We copy no density threshold, laboratory test recipe, pasteurization schedule or legal classification from this older guide. It is a professional process guide, not a home bottling shortcut.

The cider-specific IFPC pressure guidance and AWRI microbiological guidance independently support the limited finishing discussion. Lea's food-contact discussion and AWRI's copper FAQ corroborate excluding improvised metal-contact remedies. No practical chemical intervention is specified. Suspect lots are not tasted to establish safety; identifying a smell or surface appearance is not a diagnosis.

Additional discovery: PubMed record 22850371 was found for cider lactic bacteria, but opening returned no article text. It is not used as an independently read study. Indexed IFPC activity reports were not needed for any additional operating claim. No unseen article is cited as if reviewed.

## Decisions and numerical audit

- Chapter 11 reuses the conditional around-20°C example, without making it a universal optimum. Bubble behaviour is a clue, not a sugar measurement or completion criterion.
- Its **1.050 → 1.030 → 1.014** sequence is an explicitly separate, invented trend illustration. There are no assigned dates, speeds, remaining sugar calculations or packaging permissions. These are not actual readings of the ten-litre example.
- The nominal ten litres remains a starting quantity only. Sampling and transfers reduce available volume; no actual recovered amount has been invented.
- Chapters 12–14 introduce no actual fault, rescue, racking, successful maturation, malolactic completion or measured analysis for that batch.
- Chapter 15 keeps it dry and still. Sweetness, carbonation and stability are treated as separate questions. No universal priming dose, pressure target, sealed-bottle heating procedure or additive recipe appears.
- The terms dry and sweet are sensory/process intentions here, not asserted regulatory categories. Actual sale or labelling requires checking applicable definitions.
