# La dernière voix claire — French style reference

Applies to all new chapters and revisions. Established with the revision of chapters 1–2 on 10 October 2026. Read alongside the story bible, chapter plan, clue ledger and crime-thriller writing guide before drafting.

## Register and tenses

Write an absorbing contemporary French novel whose language is useful to learners. Keep the prose idiomatic, precise and accessible without flattening the characters, suspense or technical detail.

- Use **passé composé** for completed actions in the main narrative: « Daniel a ramené le curseur au début du passage. » Do not use passé simple as the default narrative tense.
- Keep **imparfait** for setting, habits, ongoing states and background: « Depuis huit heures, il écoutait ce même repas de famille. »
- Keep **plus-que-parfait** for events before the current narrative moment: « Il avait vérifié son matériel. »
- Use present, future, conditional and subjunctive naturally where meaning requires them. This is not a mechanical conversion of every verb into passé composé.
- Vary sentence structure to avoid monotonous strings of « Il a… ». Preserve clarity about who acts and when; do not solve repetition by switching arbitrarily into present-tense narration.
- Prefer familiar constructions and words where equally precise. « Ça » is welcome in narration close to Daniel's thoughts; « cela » is also acceptable when it fits the sentence. Avoid ornate inversions and needlessly literary synonyms.

## Dialogue and individual voices

Use natural spoken French with readable standard spelling. Allow « on », « ça », direct questions and occasional omission of negative « ne » where they fit the speaker. Do not remove every « ne », add slang indiscriminately, or use phonetic texting spellings.

Daniel is measured, dry, sometimes brusque, and more formally polite with clients. His wit comes from exact observation and understatement. Age does not make his French archaic. Ben can use shorter, more relaxed constructions, with a distinct voice rather than a collection of fashionable slang. Ruth's hesitations must express her particular circumstances, not make her permanently vague. Preserve the established choices of tu/vous according to each relationship.

Keep dialogue tags unobtrusive. Use action beats and untagged exchanges when the speaker is clear. Conversational language should not erase differences in age, temperament, grief or social setting. The characters and setting remain British; French idiom must not accidentally relocate the story to France.

## Tone, pacing and technical detail

Remain close to Daniel's perceptions. Show grief through habits, objects and avoided actions; avoid explanatory speeches about his emotions. Let humour emerge from friction and behaviour without weakening danger or grief.

Vary pacing deliberately: patient observation during restoration and clue examination, shorter sentences under pressure, quiet space after emotional turns. Explain technical work through a concrete task: what Daniel wants to learn, what he does, what he hears, and what the result cannot establish. Introduce specialist vocabulary in context and reuse it naturally. Retain uncertainty, comparison with originals and the risk of hearing what one expects. Never turn restoration into recovery of information absent from the recording.

Keep all clue wording, timing, ambiguity and evidence limitations consistent with the clue ledger. A language revision must not silently change the solution, sequence of discoveries or strength of an inference.

## Bilingual reader and revision checklist

- Preserve natural English equivalents: French narrative passé composé normally corresponds to English simple past here, not systematic English present perfect.
- Align compound verbs as meaningful units, including auxiliaries, negation and pronouns where needed. Check agreement, elision, accents and complete phrase coverage after edits.
- Check the chapter aloud for believable dialogue and repetitive rhythms. Keep sentence structure learnable without making every sentence equally short.
- Audit tense choices, character voice, technical accuracy and clue integrity before release.
- Treat the app's `_src/ch-N.txt` markup as the publication source. Regenerate chapter JSON and manuscript reading copies after edits. Match catalogue and chapter versions when publishing a revision.

The revised first two chapters are the starting reference for subsequent French chapters.
# Standing editorial companion

Read [the user's editorial guide](crime-mystery-editorial-guide.md) alongside this style reference before writing or revising. Direct speech uses French guillemets and English curly quotation marks, with clear speaker anchors and a new manuscript paragraph on speaker changes. Tags and actions stay outside quoted speech. Preserve the spoken register and passé composé while allowing precise imagery, organic wordplay, and Daniel's dry, sometimes self-incriminating observations. Do not explain an implication that the scene has already earned, or force humour into grief. See [the review through chapter 22](the-last-clear-voice-editorial-review.md) for the current baseline.
