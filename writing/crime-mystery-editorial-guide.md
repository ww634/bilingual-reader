# Editorial Guide: Elevating a Crime / Detective / Mystery Novel to Outstanding Quality

User-supplied guide, 14 October 2026. Standing reference for revisions and all future chapters of *La dernière voix claire*.

Use this guide when reading a manuscript chapter by chapter and then reviewing the whole. The goal is a novel that is logically airtight, atmospherically gripping, stylistically stimulating, and free of technical faults—while rewarding an attentive reader rather than spoon-feeding them.

These are editorial criteria, not a requirement to include every technique or aspect in every chapter. Let each scene's purpose determine emphasis.

## 1. Chapter-Level Pass (Read Sequentially, One Chapter at a Time)

For every chapter, check the following before moving on:

### Continuity & Coherence

- Does the chapter connect smoothly and logically with the previous one (time, place, character knowledge, physical state, emotional state, open questions)?
- Does it set up the next chapter without jarring jumps or unexplained shifts?
- Are any changes in location, time, or point of view clearly signalled and justified?

### Plot Logic & Mystery Mechanics

- Does every action and revelation follow from what the characters know (or could reasonably know) at this point?
- Are clues planted fairly? Can an attentive reader theoretically solve or at least narrow the possibilities without needing information withheld from the detective/protagonist?
- Are red herrings purposeful and later accounted for, or do they feel arbitrary?
- Does the chapter advance the central investigation, deepen a subplot, raise stakes, or meaningfully develop character/atmosphere? If it does none of these, flag it for cutting or revision.
- Note any inconsistencies in timeline, alibis, forensic detail, or police procedure.

### Pacing & Tension

- Does the chapter maintain or deliberately modulate suspense?
- Are quieter investigative or reflective passages still purposeful (character insight, atmosphere, planting of information)?
- Avoid both rushed revelations and unnecessary padding.

### Character & Dialogue

- Do characters act and speak consistently with established traits, knowledge, and motivations?
- Does dialogue advance plot, reveal character, or heighten tension while sounding natural to the speaker?
- Watch for info-dumps disguised as conversation.

### Language & Intellectual Stimulation (Style Priority)

- Demand image-rich, precise prose. Look for well-chosen similes and metaphors that illuminate character, mood, or theme rather than decorate.
- Favour descriptions that are imaginative, sensory, and thought-provoking—evoking atmosphere, psychological state, or moral complexity without overwriting.
- Seek well-used innuendo, implication, and subtext. The prose should invite the reader to infer, connect, and think rather than state the obvious. Flag any passage that spells out what a sharp reader would already understand.
- Ensure wordplay, irony, or layered meaning feels organic to voice and genre tone (wry, noir, clinical, etc.) and does not undercut tension or clarity.
- Check that heightened language never obscures vital clues or logical progression.

### Technical Cleanliness

- Correct all spelling, grammar, punctuation, and syntax errors.
- Ensure consistent tense, point of view, and narrative distance within the chapter.
- Flag awkward phrasing, repetition, or sentences that interrupt flow.

## Application to this novel

The following implements the user's accompanying requests; it is an addition to the supplied guide.

- Put direct speech in French guillemets « … » and English curly quotation marks “…”; use inner quotation marks for a quotation within speech. A speaker change starts a new manuscript paragraph. Keep speech tags and action beats outside quotation marks. Quoted documents, recordings and messages remain clearly introduced as such.
- Name the speaker when a conversation starts or resumes, when three people could be speaking, and whenever an action beat or interruption makes attribution ambiguous. Use unobtrusive tags and individual actions, not a name after every yes/no.
- Strengthen Daniel's close-third-person narration through precise observation, dry humour, occasional apt simile and understated wordplay. His ear, professional habits, impatience and capacity for self-deception supply the comparisons. Do not make every character equally epigrammatic.
- Keep grief and betrayal emotionally credible. Silence can do work that another joke or explanation would undo. Remove redundant explanations after an effective image, action or exchange.
- Preserve contemporary French, passé composé for completed narrative actions, natural dialogue, and meaningful learner alignment. Richer language does not require passé simple, gratuitous obscurity or literal English translations of French wordplay.
- Protect exact evidential quotations, dates, source histories and clue strength. A style edit must not make an uncertain inference into a fact, add undisclosed evidence, or change who knows what.
- After the sequential pass, review the whole published manuscript for cumulative repetition, voice, emotional progression, clue distribution and dangling obligations. Record remaining research/continuity questions without claiming the unfinished novel's final payoffs have been verified.
